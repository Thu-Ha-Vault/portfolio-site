### What are digital marketing and e-commerce?
#### What are digital marketing and e-commerce?
- The first sale made completely online happened in **1994**: Dan Kohn sold a CD for $12.48 over an encrypted website he built himself
- E-commerce has grown into a global, multi-billion dollar industry → generally **cheaper, more convenient, and more accessible** than storefront sales; also launched new industries and career paths like digital marketing
- **E-commerce**: the buying and selling of goods and services online; four categories:
	- **Physical products** (books, home goods)
	- **Digital products** (ebooks, online courses)
	- **Services** (vacations, doctors' appointments)
	- **Software** (word processing, photo editing programs)
- No physical storefront needed → easier to start a business → crowded market → businesses brought their ads online → digital marketing
- **Digital marketing**: the practice of reaching consumers online through digital channels with the aim of turning them into customers
	- **Digital channel**: any communication method or platform a business can use to reach their target audience online (websites, search engines, email, social media platforms)
	- Also helps businesses build trust, inspire loyalty, and better serve customers
- Advantages over traditional marketing (print advertising, billboards):
	- **Cost-effective**: serve the right ads to the right audiences at the right moments; customers can spread the word on social media
	- **Reach more people**: brand recognition in new, sometimes global, markets
	- **Faster results**: customers can take action in the moment
	- **Build relationships** through direct communication and personalization (email, social media)
- Summary: the goal of e-commerce is to generate sales; digital marketing helps do that by connecting with customers and building relationships
#### What do digital marketing and e-commerce specialists do?
- The industry is like an **engine**: many moving pieces, different parts do different jobs but work together
	- Big organization → specialize in one or two areas; smaller company → a little bit of everything
	- E.g. Joi as a small business owner: strategizing and executing marketing campaigns, website management, product development; outsources customer service and order fulfillment
- Match interests and strengths to work:
	- Writing → marketing emails, blog articles
	- Visual → image assets, website layouts
	- Organization → managing an e-commerce store
	- Numbers → pricing strategies, analyzing data for trends and customer engagement
- Job titles: **digital marketing coordinator**, **e-commerce analyst**, ... → responsibilities often overlap; share skills and digital channels (social media, email, ads)
- Common tasks: audience research, planning, creating or publishing content, updating product descriptions, analyzing data
- Goals: reach and engage potential customers, build trust, demonstrate value, drive sales, create a great customer experience
#### Transferable skills for digital marketing and e-commerce
- Three skills and qualities for success:
	- **Curiosity**: ask questions, seek answers, try new things → be curious about people (what they want, how they think, what motivates them); keep up with emerging trends in a fast-changing industry
	- **Analytical mindset**: collect and organize information to spot patterns, uncover trends, solve problems → a way of thinking, not technical skills
		- E.g. buying a car (compare cost, size, features, safety ratings), solving puzzles, tracking sports statistics to predict team performance
	- **Storytelling**: **data storytelling** turns dry numbers into engaging presentations and reports; helps businesses communicate value to customers in an accessible, engaging, memorable way
		- Not all storytelling is verbal → **visual storytelling** (drawing, design, photography)
- Other transferable skills: adaptability, leadership, **creativity** (set a brand apart, highlight its unique qualities)

#### Agency roles vs. in-house roles
- **In-house**: work for a single company to market and sell its products (e.g. Joi creates video and social content for the Think With Google brand)
	- Pros: know one company and its products extremely well; deep expertise in a specific industry; stability
	- Cons: less variety; specialized experience can make it harder to break into a new sector
- **Agency**: partners with companies that don't have in-house marketing teams to fill their digital marketing and advertising needs
	- Pros: project-based work on a variety of initiatives across multiple industries → broad, flexible expertise; can still specialize (agencies focus on areas like social media or brand building)
	- Cons: unpredictable (unexpected client requests, unfamiliar ways of working); longer hours, especially toward the end of a project
- You don't have to commit to one forever; every workplace is different
#### Roles and responsibilities in digital marketing and e-commerce
- Associate-level roles in **digital marketing**:
	- **Digital marketing coordinator**: coordinate marketing activities and initiatives, identify target audiences through research, run campaigns, monitor metrics to evaluate trends
	- **Marketing associate**: reports on marketing and sales metrics, collect and analyze customer behavior metrics (web traffic, search rankings), coordinate digital advertising materials, assist with campaigns
	- **Search Engine Optimization (SEO) specialist**: optimization strategies to improve search rankings, implement keywords, monitor SEO metrics, update links
	- **Search Engine Marketing (SEM) specialist**: identify customer personas, research keywords for SEO and **Pay Per Click (PPC)** ads, monitor metrics to optimize paid search, identify and monitor **key performance indicators (KPIs)** for SEM
	- **Social media specialist**: develop and manage a social media marketing strategy, collaborate with sales and marketing teams, create content for customer connections and brand awareness, monitor engagement metrics
	- **Email marketing specialist**: create email campaigns, build an archive of emails for lead generation, purge non-deliverable and unsubscribed addresses, monitor campaign metrics
- Associate-level roles in **e-commerce**:
	- **E-commerce analyst**: implement e-commerce strategies, set goals and KPIs (audience, acquisition, behavior, conversions), monitor and analyze metrics, leverage data for operational insights
	- **E-commerce specialist**: campaigns to increase digital sales, optimize paid advertising with SEO and other tools, manage an online marketing presence, monitor spikes in traffic or sales
- Roles to advance to:
	- **Digital marketing manager**: plan and execute SEO/SEM, email, social media, display advertising campaigns; measure performance and **ROI**; A/B tests and experiments; improve the end-to-end customer experience across channels and touchpoints
	- **Social media strategist**: oversee content, measure campaign success, recommend content optimization, network with industry professionals and influencers
	- **Account manager**: build client relationships, deliver advertising solutions on time, communicate progress to internal and external stakeholders, forecast and monitor key account metrics

#### E-commerce, ecommerce, or eCommerce?
- The course uses **e-commerce**: Google Trends shows most people prefer it, and the hyphen makes the combination of two words (electronic commerce) clearer
- Four models of e-commerce:
	- **Business-to-consumer (B2C)**: the most common form; businesses sell directly to consumers
		- First transactions: Pizza Hut (a pizza sold online) is sometimes credited, but Dan Kohn sold a CD album to a friend in Philadelphia on **August 11, 1994** using encryption
		- Specializations: **experiential marketing** (also called engagement marketing; consumers experience the brand, draws out emotional responses), **social media marketing**, **influencer marketing** (influential people endorse a brand to their followers)
	- **Consumer-to-consumer (C2C)**: individuals sell to other individuals
		- Boston Computer Exchange created the first C2C platform in **1982** (used computers); best-known early platform: **eBay** (1995); today eBay, Etsy, Amazon, Facebook Marketplace
		- Digital marketing priorities: SEO, content creation, social media (consumers find each other through blogs and social posts)
	- **Business-to-business (B2B)**: businesses sell to other businesses; accelerated by **Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)** (subscription access to business-critical services over the internet)
		- E.g. **Salesforce** (1999): **customer relationship management (CRM)** software that unifies sales, marketing, and services
		- More precise target audiences, communicate immediate value → more strategic; greater emphasis on marketing analytics and data
	- **Consumer-to-business (C2B)**: consumers sell products or services to businesses (e.g. **Upwork**, 2015, connects freelancers to businesses; influencers selling promotion services)
- Government variants: **business-to-administration (B2A)** (e.g. software for a government permitting entity) and **consumer-to-administration (C2A)** (e.g. paying utility bills)

### How digital marketing and e-commerce benefit organizations
#### Welcome to module 2

- Methods change, but some things are always important: **raising awareness, fostering trust, building loyalty**
#### How digital marketing and e-commerce create value
- Over **60%** of the global population is online → every business needs an online presence, but a website alone isn't enough: your target audience needs to know you exist, how you can help them, and what makes you different
- **Digital marketing is bigger than sales**: individual sales are easy to measure but don't mean much without trust and repeat purchases → a successful effort guides all customer interactions **before, during, and after a purchase**
- Example: a running shoe company
	- Without research: video ads on a popular news site + social media posts → little engagement, few new customers, no idea why → wasted time, money, resources
	- With audience research: ads on running-focused sites, tailored social content → meaningful goals → measure success → adjust video, social, email campaigns → sales up + awareness, trust, loyalty
- Recap: digital marketing affects customer interactions at all levels and every stage of the purchase journey; learning about customers lets teams reach their audience and nurture relationships over time
#### Advantages and challenges of digital marketing
- **Traditional marketing**: not online (printed media, television, radio, direct mail, phone calls, billboards); **digital marketing**: exclusively online (search engine results, email, video, blogs, social media)
- Advantages:
	- **Spending**: content converts easily between formats; ads shown selectively to people more likely to be interested → less wasteful spending, better return (TV or billboard ads are seen equally by interested and uninterested people)
	- **Technology**: engage and interact with customers more effectively; collect data and measure results faster → change direction easily; adapts to new technologies
	- **Target audiences**: expand reach beyond traditional marketing; tailor ad visibility and audience coverage
- Challenges (one for each advantage):
	- **Spending**: content is easier to create, but **omnichannel marketing** (integration or synchronization of content on multiple channels for a consistent brand experience) needs much more content, and it must be distinctive; costs of all channels add up → hard to get budget buy-in
	- **Technology**: constantly learn new tools; follow user privacy and data sharing regulations worldwide; omnichannel makes it harder to track where sales come from → rely on analytics tools
	- **Target audiences**: the digital space is crowded and getting more crowded → harder to stand out even when targeting the right audiences

### Introduction to the marketing funnel
#### The customer journey and journey maps
- An effective marketing strategy starts with learning about customers' needs and **pain points** (the specific problems customers and potential customers want to solve)
	- Ask "How do we help customers address their pain points or achieve their goals?" instead of "How do we sell more products?"
- **Customer journey**: the path from learning about a product → getting questions answered → making a purchase (and recommending it)
- Example: a new weather app → search "best weather apps" → a familiar name (a friend or an online ad) → read reviews → pick the one topping best-of lists with a trial subscription → app doesn't work → live chat support fixes it quickly → after a month's trial, an email reminder → buy a subscription → loyal customer
- **Touchpoint**: each interaction with the brand during the purchase journey → each can help or hurt the impression; a bad experience can mean an abandoned journey
- **Customer journey map**: a visualization of the touchpoints a typical customer encounters along their purchase journey
	- Can have multiple maps for different types of customers; not about predicting the future but understanding how and why customers interact with the business → better customer experiences
#### What is a touchpoint?
- Touchpoints reveal the decisions customers make during their journey; they occur on the website or mobile app and on all media channels before a customer discovers the website
- **Touchpoints relate to a specific context or need**: don't classify "social media" as a touchpoint; classify e.g. a customer's response to a **flash sale announcement on Twitter** (context: curiosity; need: saving money)
- **Touchpoints are customer-centric**: a business-centric view turns them into a calculation (ratio of touchpoints to purchases); a customer-centric view gives more insight into buyer motivations
- Define touchpoints carefully for all media channels → most useful data about customer motivations, preferences, behaviors
- What is a touchpoint?: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/gFB3O
#### The marketing funnel
- **Marketing funnel** (also **sales funnel** or **conversion funnel**; used for over 100 years): a visual representation of the process through which people go from first learning about a brand to becoming loyal customers
	- Wide at the top, narrow at the bottom: many enter, some reach the bottom; people drop off at every stage
- Four stages:
	- **Awareness**: a potential customer encounters the brand for the first time (an ad, a recommendation) → raise as much awareness as possible among the **target audience** (the group of people most likely to purchase a company's products)
	- **Consideration**: potential customers think about doing business (browsing the website, comparing brands) → make a good impression
	- **Conversion**: someone decides to make a purchase and become a customer → demonstrate value, user-friendly experience
	- **Loyalty**: give customers reasons to return → trust, repeat business for years
- Funnel vs. journey map (best used together):
	- Journey map = the customer's perspective, traces specific touchpoints; complex, unique paths that can repeat or loop back
	- Funnel = the business's perspective, its plan for moving customers along; a simpler **linear** model of broad stages
- Weather app example: online ad and search results → awareness; trial membership → consideration; reminder email → conversion
#### The traditional marketing funnel to the digital marketing funnel
- Funnels have been revised over time to reflect changes in business, technology, and customer behavior
- **ToFU / MoFU / BoFU**: Top / Middle / Bottom of Funnel → aim for the most desirable outcomes at each
- **Traditional sales funnel**: Awareness (ToFU) → Interest (MoFU: a top choice when researching) → Decision (MoFU: chosen over competitors) → Action (BoFU: purchase)
- **Combined marketing and sales funnel** (traditional and digital marketing inputs at the top): Awareness → Interest → Desire (motivation or incentive to purchase) → Action (first purchase) → Conversion (regular purchases, customer relationship established)
- **Digital marketing funnel**: Awareness and engagement (ToFU) → Consideration (MoFU) → **Remarketing** (MoFU: re-engage customers who engaged before but didn't convert; new customers go straight from consideration to conversion) → Conversion (BoFU: first purchase) → Retention (BoFU: regular purchases)
- Other variations:
	- **E-commerce marketing funnel**: Awareness → Consideration (build the brand) → Differentiation (stand out in the category) → Purchase (reach shoppers most likely to buy) → Brand readiness (return purchases)
	- **Social media marketing funnel**: Awareness → Consideration → Action → Engagement (stay top-of-mind after purchase) → **Advocacy** (customers recommend the brand)
	- **Content marketing funnel**: Awareness (web pages, blogs, social posts, infographics, podcasts) → Evaluation (surveys, email, webinars, educational events) → Conversion (customer success stories, webinars, specifications, demos)
- Key takeaways: no single funnel fits all needs; funnels exist for general sales and marketing and for specialized areas; the program mostly refers to the four-stage funnel (awareness, consideration, conversion, loyalty)
- The traditional marketing funnel to the digital marketing funnel: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/rFur7
### Attract and engage potential customers
#### The top of the funnel: Awareness and consideration
- **Awareness**: get the brand in front of the right audience
	- First step: **research** → who your customers are and where they are online → pick the channels most likely to reach them
	- Tactics: improve search engine rankings (optimize website content around specific search terms), paid digital ads (websites, social media, email, video platforms), promotional partnerships with other brands or influencers
	- A strong top-of-funnel strategy uses **multiple channels** with a **consistent experience** across them
	- Awareness isn't a one-time event: it can take days, weeks, or longer to reach consideration → goal: get them to remember you (e.g. a refrigerator is a big, infrequent purchase → a consistent online presence reminds them when their old fridge breaks)
- **Consideration**: the "getting to know you" stage → increase the chances of an eventual purchase (they read reviews, browse the website)
	- **Lead**: a potential customer who has interacted with a brand and shared personal information, like an email address
	- Re-engage visitors who left by serving ads based on the products and pages they visited
	- Educate about offerings and how they address pain points: blog posts, newsletters, webinars; free samples, tools, trial memberships → build trust, help an informed choice
#### Case study: How MERSEA structures its marketing funnel
- **MERSEA**: lifestyle brand in Lenexa, Kansas (founded 2013 by Lina Dickinson and Melanie Bolin) → clothing, accessories, home fragrances inspired by travel and seaside escapes; first sold through retail partners (**over 1,200 stores**)
- Challenge: increase e-commerce sales → acquire new customers online and turn them into repeat customers
- Approach: online paid advertising, email marketing, social media marketing, text messaging; hired an outside marketing company for Google Ads (requires platform knowledge and metric interpretation) → founders focus on products
- By funnel stage:
	- **Awareness**: Google Ads (**Search ads** for brand name and product searches, **Shopping ads** for specific product types like "kimono sweater") and Facebook Ads (lifestyle-based targeting)
	- **Consideration**: **remarketing** (ads for the products people viewed on the website); storytelling about products on social media and email
	- **Conversion**: follow-up emails for **abandoned carts** (a customer adds an item to the cart but doesn't purchase) → higher conversion rate
	- **Loyalty**: emails about new products twice a week (one weekday, one weekend); text messages for order delivery confirmation and updates → more personal relationship
- Results: within two years, online sales rose from **10% to 45%**; around **50%** of customers are returning customers
- No one correct way to structure a funnel → try and test new ideas; a well-made product + a well-rounded marketing approach
- Case study: How MERSEA structures its marketing funnel: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/Tbqs4
#### Measuring success at the top of the funnel
- Measuring outcomes at each stage shows what works, what doesn't, and where to improve
- **Metric**: a quantifiable measurement used to track and assess progress toward an objective
- Awareness metrics:
	- **Impressions**: total number of times an ad appears on people's screens (including repeats) → an ad seen 200 times by 100 people = 200 impressions
	- **Reach**: total number of unique individuals who encounter an ad across their devices → = 100
	- **Frequency**: how many times each individual encounters the ad in a given timeframe → each person twice a week = 2
	- More specific goals are possible (e.g. a number of visitors to a web page) → set goals for each stage
- Consideration metrics → **engagement**: how much, how often, how long
	- How often customers search for the business, first-time visitors, pages per visitor, time on page, email or newsletter sign-ups (contact info to remind them later)
	- More engagement isn't always better (e.g. if customers take too long to complete tasks → re-evaluate the website layout)
- Measuring awareness and engagement is often harder than measuring bottom-of-funnel results like sales
### Drive sales and build brand loyalty
#### The bottom of the funnel: Conversion and loyalty
- **Conversion stage** (also **decision** or **purchase** stage): someone makes a purchase and becomes a customer
	- Customers buy when one company's solution fits their needs better → demonstrate and deliver value, build trust and confidence
	- Different customers reach conversion through different touchpoints, but trends in behavior can be identified → personalize content
	- Stand out with clear, useful content and experiences: accurate product descriptions, a smooth checkout process, a clear returns policy, well-placed product-focused ads, a clearly organized website → easy to find answers and take action
- **Loyalty stage**: balance customer **acquisition** with **retention**
	- Depends foremost on product quality and value, but loyalty comes from a great customer experience **during and after purchase**
	- Follow-up emails, rewards programs, social media engagement → always purposeful and thoughtful (e.g. don't send a coupon for something a customer just bought)
	- A positive post-purchase experience turns customers into devoted fans
#### Measuring success at the bottom of the funnel
- **Conversion**: a potential customer takes the desired action (here, a purchase)
- Conversion metrics: number of conversions, **time to conversion**, **cost per conversion**, average number of touchpoints before conversion, **average order size** → address drop-off issues
	- **Cart abandonment**: a customer adds an item to their cart but doesn't complete the purchase → nearly **70%** of online carts are abandoned → find where customers drop off in checkout
- Loyalty metrics (depend on audience and goals): rate of repeat purchases, length of time between purchases, number of orders per customer, rate of account activation after sign-up, engagement with rewards programs
- Customer loyalty is the truest test of how well the whole funnel served customers → improve retention rates, build revenue over time
#### The future of e-commerce
- Trends for the next decade:
	- **Specialization of smaller retailers**: large marketplaces will still account for most purchases; smaller retailers offer highly customizable products, niche categories, more personalized service
	- **Immersive customer experiences**:
		- **Virtual reality (VR)**: fully immersive simulated world with a headset (e.g. a virtual dressing room)
		- **Augmented reality (AR)**: semi-immersive, adds audio-visual feedback to your existing world (e.g. sunglasses overlaid on an image of yourself on your phone)
		- **Chatbots**: computer programs designed to simulate conversation with human users (an online sales associate); AI and machine learning on real conversation data may improve the shopping experience
	- **Distribution and delivery innovations**: drones or self-driving vehicles for the **last-mile** trip from warehouse to home or office
- E-commerce will continue to grow, and daily habits will change too

## Module 3: Digital marketing and e-commerce strategy
### How companies use digital marketing to build their brands
#### Welcome to module 3
- Module 3: how a funnel fits into a larger **digital marketing strategy** → branding and digital marketing → planning a strategy → common channels and tactics (SEO, social media marketing, email marketing)
- Strategies ensure a brand's digital marketing and sales activities all work toward a **common goal** (Joi's first work as a product marketing manager: collaborating on social media strategies)
#### The value of brands for digital marketing
- **Brand**: how a business or organization is perceived by the public → influenced by values, culture, design elements, tone of voice
	- Not a physical asset like products or services; more like a **personality or reputation** → the sum of who you are and what people think about you
- A company's brand guides all marketing and sales efforts → first understand the mission, values, unique offerings
	- Marketing changes with the times, but **brand identity is permanent**: "If a brand is who you are, marketing is how you get people's attention"
	- Brand = the foundation of a digital marketing strategy; digital marketing also affects public perception of the brand → you can't have one without the other
- **Brand equity**: the value consumers attribute to one brand's offering compared with similar products from another brand
	- E.g. two sunscreens with identical ingredients → customers pay more for the known and trusted brand
	- Digital marketing builds that trust: communicates the brand's values, personality, perspective → brand equity, recognition, trust
#### Brand safety
- **Brand safety**: the practice of keeping a brand's reputation safe when it advertises online → ads not placed next to or on inappropriate or inaccurate content; no copyrighted materials used without permission
	- E.g. display network ads on a misinformation site damage the brand; an inclusive brand shouldn't appear on sites with hateful views
- The **Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB)** lists **13 topics** to avoid: military conflict, obscenity, drugs, tobacco, adult content, arms, crime, death/injury, online piracy, hate speech, terrorism, spam, fake news (+ brand-specific ones, e.g. baby toys shouldn't appear on gambling sites)
- Ensuring brand safety: define what is "unsafe" for your brand → scale isn't everything → use trusted technology (e.g. opt out of specific sites on the Google Display Network)
- Strategies:
	- **Buy ad space directly from reputable publishers** (may miss out on potential sales)
	- **Image recognition** to identify unsafe content
	- **Select keywords to avoid**
	- **Geotargeting**: know which regions your ads run in → stay sensitive and relevant
- Resources: Facebook brand safety controls; Google Display & Video 360 brand safety targeting
- Brand safety: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/O9oyE
### Create a digital marketing and e-commerce strategy
#### The elements of a digital marketing strategy
- Entry-level roles usually help **execute** a strategy (setting it is a broad collaborative effort, unless at a small company)
- **Digital marketing strategy**: a plan for achieving specific goals through online channels to support and advance business objectives
- Steps:
	1. **Research your audience** and **set meaningful goals** (who you're trying to reach, what you want to accomplish)
	2. Work out the strategy: guide customers through each stage of the funnel with the most effective tactics and **media mix** (the combination of digital channels used to reach your goals and how you divide your budget among them)
		- Review existing media content for gaps → decide which channels to focus on, what content and campaigns to run, how to allocate the budget
	3. Create the content (email templates, video animations, ad copy, banner images)
	4. Plan to **measure and analyze** results
- Example: a high-end jewelry retailer wants a **30% increase in revenue within three years**; jewelry customers take a long time from awareness to purchase → upper and middle funnel goals (new customers, nurture leads)
	- **40%** of budget → upper funnel: video ads (most effective for awareness among the target audience)
	- **40%** → middle funnel: engage across social media + paid ads for people actively researching jewelry
	- **20%** → product-focused ads to website visitors + email marketing to convert nurtured leads
#### Find your audience and understand your customers
- Knowing your audience (geographic location, interests, online activities, preferences, how they like to consume content) → campaigns that interest and engage them → brand growth, awareness
- **Customer personas**: represent a group of similar people in a desirable audience; profiles of likely customers based on data and research
	- Reach people at the right time with the right message, offer, or products; focus on prospective leads, not random people
	- Created with marketing automation tools with persona generators (**HubSpot**, **Xtensio**, **Up Close & Persona**) or manually
- Research via surveys, interviews, existing data in automation tools → demographics (gender, age, geographical location, income, education, job type); ask questions based on business goals (personality traits, hobbies, social media platforms used)
- Find your audience and understand your customers: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/OciIN
#### Define your marketing goals
- Every company, department, team, and employee benefits from clear, relevant goals
- **Business goals**: desired aims, achievements, or outcomes for a business → big, long-term, can affect the whole company (increase profits, gain new customers, improve customer service, raise productivity, launch new products)
- **Marketing goals**: smaller, more targeted objectives in a marketing plan that support business aims (raise brand awareness, increase web traffic, generate new leads, drive sales or conversions)
- Both must be **specific and measurable**; example: photo editing software company
	- Business goal: "Grow our number of active customers by 25% over the next two years by adding five frequently-requested features to our software" (what, how, when)
	- Website traffic doesn't necessarily equal new users → better: lead generation ("increase our lead generation by 40% in the next year by running upper-funnel ads that highlight our new features and increasing our mid-funnel budget by 20%")
- Business goals are like the **top of a pyramid**: many marketing goals below support them; a strategy can have multiple goals, plus goals for each part (social media, email)
#### How to set SMART goals
- **SMART**: specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, time-bound → organized, a sense of direction, a time frame
	- **Specific**: What do you want to accomplish?
	- **Measurable**: What success metrics determine whether the objective has been met?
	- **Attainable**: Is this a realistic goal?
	- **Relevant**: Is it aligned with the company's overall objectives?
	- **Time-bound**: What is a realistic time frame?
- Example (photography and photo sharing company, scrapbooks): "We aim to increase scrapbook sales by 15% by the end of the next quarter through a robust social media campaign where we focus heavily on influencer marketing."
- How to set SMART goals: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/Lfm7D
### Reach customers online
#### Paid, owned, and earned media
- All digital channels fit into three categories; a strong strategy uses all of them
- **Paid media**: any form of digital promotion a brand pays to put online (banner ads, video ads, social media ads, shopping ads, pop-ups)
	- Pros: fast results; drives traffic to owned and earned channels too
	- Cons: results disappear when you stop paying; can get expensive over time
- **Owned media**: all the digital content a brand fully controls (website content, blogs, eBooks, social media content, whitepapers, case studies)
	- Doesn't sell directly; gives customers what they need to make informed decisions → "If paid ads bring consumers to a company's website, owned content is what convinces them to stay"
	- Can't do much on its own → bring customers via paid ads and high search rankings
- **Earned media**: any positive digital exposure generated through personal or public recommendations → companies don't create, own, or pay for it directly
	- Generated by customers in response to product quality, customer service, successful campaigns (a formal influencer partnership = paid; a customer telling friends for free = earned)
	- Forms: social media mentions, blog posts, written or video reviews, positive press (product profiles, best-of lists) → reach new markets, build public trust
#### Attract customers with search engine optimization
- Search engines explore and index billions of webpages and use an **algorithm** to rank results by relevance and quality; the most common way people find products and services online
	- E.g. "potted cactus for sale" → over **26 million** results (more than two million pages) → ranking is essential
- **Search engine optimization (SEO)**: a set of practices designed to increase the quantity and quality of traffic to a website → optimize content around commonly used search terms (**keywords**)
- **Search engine results pages (SERPs)**: the pages of results produced when someone performs a search → the higher a site ranks, the more likely customers click
- SEO vs. paid ads: SEO exposure lasts much longer but rising in rankings takes time and effort → often used together
- SEO basics:
	- **Keyword research**: identifying common search terms and phrases consumers use to find products or services (e.g. a musical instrument company: "guitar", "drum set", "grand piano for sale")
	- **Quality content**: useful, usable, up to date, matches current offerings and keyword trends (don't just pack the site with keywords)
	- **Website structure**: well organized, user-friendly → easier for search engines and customers to navigate
#### Reach customers with search engine marketing
- **Search engine marketing (SEM)**: the process of generating traffic to a website through paid ads that appear on SERPs (above or below the regular listings)
	- Terminology: some treat SEM as an umbrella covering paid search ads and SEO; in this course SEO = ranking of listings, SEM = paid ads
- Starts with keyword research (e.g. a vitamin company bids on "vitamin C") → **ad auction**: how a search engine determines which ads to display and their order when someone searches
	- **Pay-per-click (PPC)**: you pay only when someone clicks your ad; **cost-per-click (CPC)**: the amount paid per click
	- You can lose a bid; search engines decide based on factors like quality and maximum bid amount
- Advantages: reach specific customers quickly; control the **landing page** (sign-up form, specific product page instead of the homepage); **remarketing**
	- **Remarketing**: identify previous customers or website visitors and serve them paid ads on other sites and social media (e.g. jeans you browsed popping up on other sites) → most visitors leave before completing an action; they are the segment most likely to convert
#### Display advertising
- **Display ads**: visual ad formats (images, text, videos, GIFs) placed on webpages or apps through display networks
- **Display network**: a group of websites, videos, and apps where your ads can appear (some include up to **two million websites**); target by audience, context, location, ... (e.g. **Google Display Network**, **Taboola**, **Criteo**)
- **Ad exchanges**: digital marketplaces where buyers and sellers enter a **real-time bidding** process for ad space; pull inventory from multiple publishers and display networks; advertisers bid on impressions via a **demand-side platform** (e.g. Xandr, OpenX, Magnite, Pubmatic, Google Ad Manager)
- Types of display ads:
	- **Image ads**: static JPG, PNG, or animated GIF
	- **Text ads**: text only, least dynamic
	- **Responsive ads**: automatically adjust size and format to the available space; can appear as **native ads** (look like a native piece of the website's content)
	- **App promotion ads**: drive app downloads and engagement → send users straight to the app store; only on compatible devices
- In the funnel: awareness and consideration (drive awareness, grow the customer base); further down for **remarketing** (ads to people who visited your website or social profile) → conversions, loyal customers
- Display advertising: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/yjfPx
### Engage customers through social media and email marketing
#### Introduction to social media and email marketing
- Social media and email have different strengths → more effective together
- **Social media**: one of the most effective channels for finding new audiences → engage customers, run ads, generate earned media through viral content
	- Drawback: brands control their posts but not the platforms → a platform shutting down or changing its algorithm can cut reach overnight
	- Social media helps grow **email lists**, which brands own completely
- **Email marketing**: nurtures relationships with people already interested in the brand; incredibly cost-effective → on average **more than $40 for every dollar spent**
#### Social media marketing basics
- Well over half the people in the world are on social media
- **Social media marketing**: the practice of creating and publishing content for social media platforms to drive engagement and promote a brand or product → same concerns as the overall strategy (engagement, reach, brand), on social platforms
- **Five pillars of social media marketing**:
	1. **Strategy**: know your audience and which platforms they use (e.g. a home decorating audience → not a business networking platform); know your goals (build community → interactive content like polls or Q&A; promote a new product → contest or giveaway)
	2. **Planning and publishing**: when and how often to post → engage enough to be remembered without overwhelming followers
	3. **Listening and engagement**: track and analyze social conversations and mentions (direct responses, earned media) → decide how to respond; like a conversation: publish → listen → reply (e.g. a habit tracking app: thanks for praise, troubleshooting tips for problems)
	4. **Analytics and reporting**: measure comments, shares, follows, clicks, hashtag use, positive mentions over time → respond to trends, guide future strategies
	5. **Paid social media**: expand reach, remarket; more detailed user information than search engines → highly specific customer groups; link ads to email sign-up forms → grow email lists
#### Choose the right platforms for your target audience
- Using platforms as a digital marketer differs from personal use → choose platforms by goal
- **Goal 1: Build connections with your audience** → interactive, word-based platforms with short messages and open communication like **X** (a brand can post dozens of times a day without overloading followers)
	- Be responsive and engaging; empathetic customer service for order issues; stay authentic to the brand voice
	- Pro tip: be proactive, seek out authentic connections in your community
- **Goal 2: Target new customers** → image-based photo and video platforms like **Instagram** (show, don't tell; tell brand and product stories with dynamic visuals)
	- Users spend time learning, getting inspired, shopping; **influencer marketing** introduces your brand to new followers; social media ad campaigns increase reach
	- Pro tip: choose influencer partners whose audiences will be interested in your product
- **Goal 3: Drive traffic to your website** → platforms for sharing articles, links, products, images, reviews like **Facebook**; fresh, informative content that links to the landing page; cohesive brand voice and visuals
	- Pro tip: not all platforms make sharing links easy
- Prioritize platforms by goals, but the same campaigns can run on several channels with content customized for each audience
- Choose the right platforms for your target audience: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/9Znon
#### Build relationships with email marketing
- **Spam** (junk mail): any unwanted emails sent out in bulk to a mass recipient list (too-good-to-be-true claims, same content to everyone, too many emails)
- **Email marketing**: a brand sends messages to a list of existing subscribers to share information, drive sales, or create community → relevant, helpful content to people who agreed to receive it
- **Email segmentation**: dividing a subscriber list into smaller groups based on criteria like interests, location, purchase history → relevant content per group
	- E.g. a pet store: a cat food buyer appreciates cat treats or toys; a birdseed coupon may get deleted or cause an unsubscribe
- **Personalization**: customizing email content for individual subscribers:
	- Name in the greeting or subject line
	- Promotions for birthdays, holidays, anniversaries
	- Messages matching the customer's funnel stage (no loyalty program emails for people still in consideration)
	- Follow up on previous interactions (ask for an opinion on a recent purchase)
#### Types of email campaigns
- Email marketing involves testing tactics to learn what works for your audience and goals
- **Acquisition emails**: acquire new customers before they consider the brand → **awareness**; usually offer something free (downloadable guide, free trial, educational material)
- **Welcome emails**: to brand new customers or subscribers → **consideration** (deeper engagement, specific actions)
- **Newsletters**: sent regularly with news and informational content → versatile: consideration, conversion, and loyalty
- **Promotional emails**: inform subscribers of new or existing products or services → consideration and loyalty (encourage action)
- **Retention emails**: to current customers to keep them as customers → **loyalty**
- Types of email campaigns: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/NvKrS

## Module 4: Measure performance success
### Measure marketing performance success
#### Welcome to module 4
- Module 4 is all about **data**: how you measure it, how you interpret it, how it makes your strategy better
	- **Performance marketing**, working with data in an entry-level role, using data to find what works and adapt to change, **data storytelling** to demonstrate findings
- Measuring the success of marketing efforts may be the most important thing in a digital marketing or e-commerce career → measuring results can always make a strategy better (brand building, engaging customers, maximizing sales)
- Instructor's example: running paid ads for her own e-commerce store to see if the extra cost was worth it → a whole world of measurement beyond paid campaigns (dashboards, analytics for the health of the whole store) → you can prove your tactics help reach your goals
#### Measure progress with performance marketing
- Example: 1985, a marketing agency promoting a new breakfast cereal → focus groups + past experience → ads in newspapers, billboards, TV → sales go up, but you don't know which ads were most effective or how many new customers came through the campaign (limited information)
- Same campaign online today → dozens of ways to measure success (e.g. how many people encounter and engage with your ad each week) → rethink a weak strategy or make a good one stronger
- **Performance marketing**: the process of using concrete information about customer behaviors to plan and refine marketing and sales strategies; focuses on measurable results like clicks and conversions → set specific goals, use metrics to find out if you reached them
- Performance metrics:
	- Impressions, cost per click on paid ads
	- **Customer lifetime value**: the average revenue generated by customers over a certain period of time
	- **ROAS (return on ad spend)**: how much revenue is gained vs. how much was spent → spend $100 on an ad, make $150 → ROAS = 150%; e.g. a goal of increasing overall revenue for the cereal → ROAS as a success metric
- The average customer journey takes about **six touchpoints** (doubled more than twice over a 15-year period) → performance marketing gauges how each touchpoint contributes to goals
#### Common metrics for success
- Like tracking progress toward reading a book a month for 12 months → the same practice applies to measuring marketing campaign effectiveness
- **Metric**: a quantifiable measurement used to track and assess a business objective → helps determine the success of marketing initiatives and campaigns
- **Why track metrics?** Gauge how close you are to meeting goals; each metric tells something different, e.g. **return on investment (ROI)**, **return on ad spend (ROAS)**, cost per sale, online and sales revenue
- **Metrics in the marketing funnel**:
	- **Awareness**: gather audience data, develop user personas (who your customers are)
	- **Consideration**: cost of acquisitions, click-through rates
	- **Conversion**: sales conversion rates, average order values, cart abandonment rates
	- **Loyalty**: customer retention rate, customer lifetime value
- Key takeaway: metrics gauge effectiveness and audience contentment during a campaign and give insights for future campaigns
- Common metrics for success: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/MUDws
#### Working with data
- Performance marketing generates a lot of data: impressions and clicks at the top of the funnel → conversion and sales numbers at the bottom
- **Data**: a collection of facts or information (e.g. total social media followers, hours a team spends on a project, total yearly revenue)
	- Answers questions concretely from real customer behaviors → plan campaigns, predict future behaviors, check whether you reach your KPIs
- **KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**: a measurement used to gauge how successful a business is in its effort to reach a business or marketing goal (e.g. ROAS); not reaching goals → maybe prioritize different KPIs
- **Data analytics**: the process of monitoring and evaluating data to gain actionable insights → one of the most important skill sets; no need to be a statistics expert, but most entry-level roles involve working with data
- Main data analytics responsibilities:
	- **Data pulling**: collecting data from analytics tools and putting it into a spreadsheet or database → easy to access; e.g. campaigns with similar goals on Facebook, Bing, and Google → pull from each source into one spreadsheet to compare
	- **Data reporting** (performance reporting): organizing and summarizing data to track performance across marketing and sales efforts → identify trends, spot unexpected results faster (e.g. which source has a higher ROAS); quality reporting gives a clear picture of raw numbers and shapes questions for analysis
	- **Data analysis**: examining data to draw conclusions, make predictions, and drive informed decision-making → reporting is the **what**, analysis is the **why** → insights explain results and suggest next steps (shift budget, prioritize different KPIs)
#### Data ethics
- Knowing how to work with user data responsibly and legally is critical to the integrity of your organization, role, and projects
- **Data ethics**: the study and evaluation of moral challenges related to data collection and analysis → businesses apply ethical practices to:
	1. **Follow regulations**: many countries have laws on generating, recording, curating, processing, sharing, and using personally identifiable data
		- **Personally identifiable data (PID)**: information that can be used to directly identify, contact, or locate an individual
		- **Data privacy**: the proper handling of data → collect, process, analyze, share, archive, delete according to the data privacy laws of the countries where your customers reside; know your organization's data security and privacy protocols
	2. **Protect customer data** → **Data anonymization**: techniques to mask or remove personal information from data to protect people's identities; often on data from multiple sources; anonymized data can be shared more widely in an organization
		- Often anonymized: names, telephone numbers, email addresses, photographs, account numbers, purchase transactions
	3. **Use data fairly and without bias**: data only for legitimate business purposes
		- **Data bias**: a type of human error that skews results in a certain direction
		- Not data bias: selecting data from a target audience (e.g. customers aged 21–45)
		- Data bias: excluding data from customers who returned products because you don't consider them loyal
		- Even with all available data: historical data from an unrepresentative audience → campaigns based on past behaviors can unknowingly perpetuate a bias
		- Pro tip: ask for peer review of critical data → incorporate different perspectives
- Data ethics: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/yGGPc
### Use data insights to improve a strategy
#### Attribution models for digital marketing
- What does good performance mean (many clicks, a viral Tweet, more website traffic)? → depends on marketing and business goals, but every marketer wants to know which touchpoints get customers to take action
	- E.g. an art supplies company: many clicks on an ad for a new line of paints but few purchases → the ad might not be so successful
- You can't know what customers think, only what they do → behaviors show where efforts succeed or fall short
- **Attribution**: the process of determining which content and channels are responsible for generating leads, conversions, or sign-ups
	- Most analytics tools can find which touchpoints and keywords customers interact with before taking action → informed decisions on where to invest time, budget, resources
- The last touchpoint doesn't always deserve all the credit: average journey ~6 touchpoints, not straightforward
	- E.g. buying a computer: decide on a model after the 2nd touchpoint, put off the purchase (waiting for a holiday or a sale, distracted) → another touchpoint reminds them → both deserve credit
- **Attribution models**:
	- **Data-driven attribution**: measures customer engagement with marketing content across channels to understand what motivates action; assigns credit to each touchpoint based on statistics (which ads or keywords most often lead to conversions) → needs enough meaningful data
	- **First click attribution**: all credit to the first touchpoint that eventually leads to a conversion (e.g. a social media ad for oil paints)
	- **Last click attribution**: all credit to the last known touchpoint before conversion (e.g. a promotional email at the 4th–5th touchpoint)
	- **Linear attribution**: equal credit to each touchpoint along the customer journey
- Attribution isn't an exact science, but helps put resources where they maximize conversions
#### Data storytelling basics
- Data pulling, reporting, analysis, and attribution produce valuable insights → how to turn them into action and persuade key people? → tell a story with data
- Without a story, data is just numbers: they tell **what** happened, not **why**, why it's important, and what you can do about it
- **Data storytelling**: the practice of conveying data insights to a specific audience using a clear and compelling narrative
	- No two people tell exactly the same story (own perspectives, experiences, biases)
- A data story has **three main components** (like a play):
	1. **Data** = the characters: be selective about which numbers to highlight (lead characters, supporting roles, some not in the scene)
		- Understand the question you're trying to answer → decide which data points matter
		- E.g. a toy company measuring a new social media campaign: "Is this campaign more efficient than the last one?" → main data point: **return on ad spend (ROAS)**
	2. **Narrative** = the plot: well-structured → engaging, memorable, persuasive
		- E.g. explain how the ROAS compares to the previous campaign and how results impact future efforts
		- Explain what insights mean, why they matter to your audience, what they can do about them; you don't need all the answers, the best stories create space for discussion
	3. **Visualizations** = costumes, lighting, stage set: focus attention, help the audience understand the narrative
		- **Data visualizations**: graphic representations of data that convey information (charts, graphs, infographics, other illustrations) → clarify trends, express relationships between data points
		- E.g. compare the current vs. last campaign's ROAS with a **bar graph** instead of a series of numbers
- Data storytelling is in high demand for digital marketing and e-commerce roles → engage audiences, communicate insights, influence big decisions
#### Story structure
- Analyze the data first to find the story hidden within it → every data story has three elements: **context**, **complication**, **resolution**
- **Context**: the framing of the situation, the starting point
	- E.g. sales and customer base decreased last year → this year's goals: maximize profits and grow the customer base
- **Complication**: typically the reason the situation requires action; not always bad, an explanation of how the situation changed based on data
	- E.g. on track to meet the profit goal but not the customer base goal; any change in metrics is a complication
- **Resolution**: the necessary action taken to solve a problem or leverage an opportunity → How did the story end? What were the results of the complication? What do the results tell you about the campaign?
	- E.g. maximizing profits but not driving new customers → explain the data-driven action to change that
- **Example story** (toy company social media campaign):
	- Context: the past campaign aimed to increase sales by **5%** over **three months** with paid social media ads → didn't reach the goal → review the current campaign's data
	- Complication: compared with past ROAS and conversion rates → both increased (a favorable complication)
	- Resolution: why ROAS and conversion rates are soaring (changed copy, switched images, ran a promotion, or something else) → your findings
- Story structure: https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/supplement/fpJZ5
