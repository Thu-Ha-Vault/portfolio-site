### Understanding social media marketing
#### Welcome to module 1
- **Social media**: any digital tool that enables users to create and share content publicly
	- Billions of people on social media → your current and potential customers are likely among them
	- People use Facebook, Twitter, YouTube, Instagram to stay in touch with friends and family, share photos and videos, research products to buy, stay up to date on current events, find entertaining content
- Module 1 roadmap: what social media marketing is and its benefits → **five key pillars**: strategy, planning and publishing, listening and engagement, analytics and reporting, paid social media → types of digital media in a campaign → guide customers through the **marketing funnel** (path from awareness of your brand to purchasing a product and beyond)
#### The benefits of social media marketing
- **Social media marketing**: the process of creating content for different social media platforms in order to drive engagement and promote a business or product → connect with customers and help them better understand your brand
- Why social media is effective:
	- **Increase brand awareness**: **brand awareness** = how familiar people are with a particular business or product
		- Social media platforms engage with over half of the world's population; per a Pew Research study, over **70%** of users check their accounts at least once a day → natural place to target new customers
	- **Build stronger relationships** with existing customers: build trust, help solve problems, show you care about their needs and opinions
	- **Drive more traffic to your website**: share website content in posts; include your website address in all social media profiles
	- **Generate leads**: a **lead** is a potential customer; on social media, someone who took a specific action (filling out a form, subscribing to a free trial, clicking on a link to a discount code) → many platforms have ad formats designed to collect leads
	- **Gain valuable insights**: audience likes, dislikes, interests; platform analytics tools; learn about **competitors** (emulate what works, improve on what doesn't)
	- **Cost-effective**: most platforms are free to join; target users by interests, hobbies, and personal/demographic information → avoid spending budget on a wider audience
- Cindy's example at Google: launched a new product feature → social media shared digestible information, increased awareness, and guided users to a destination (Google Ads Help Center)
- Big companies: team of designers, copywriters, or an agency; small companies: more work yourself or a small in-house team
#### Cindy - A day in the life of a product marketing manager
- Three key areas in a typical day:
	1. Work with research agencies or sellers to understand what customers are thinking (new challenges, what's top of mind)
	2. Work with **product managers** to understand new innovations coming to market (digest technical papers and jargon, schedule time to learn the ins and outs of the product) → communicate and connect to foster relationships
	3. Develop the **marketing strategy**: incorporate earlier conversations into a document that structures the rest of the week or month
- Working across more (and more complex) products sharpens communication skills and the ability to take a product to market
- Career path: from **individual contributor** to **team leader** (manage other product marketing managers, more strategy, more cross-functional work)
- Advice: be curious and hungry to ask questions; be proactive, with a drive and desire to learn
#### The five core pillars of social media marketing
1. **Strategy**: defines the primary goals of the campaign (boost brand awareness, drive website traffic and sales, provide customer support, build a community, ...) → depends on broader marketing and business goals
	- Which **platforms** to use → research those most visited by your target audience
	- Which **types of content** your audience values (educational, inspiring, entertaining) and **formats** (images, videos, articles) → research plus a lot of trial and error
2. **Planning and publishing**: a **consistent presence** lets your brand be seen and discovered
	- Schedule posts in advance; consider **timing** (when the audience is most likely to engage) and **frequency** (stay in their minds without overwhelming their feeds)
	- **Social media scheduling tools** publish content automatically; social media calendar
3. **Listening and engagement**: as your following grows, mentions increase (comments, tags, direct messages)
	- **Social listening**: tracking and analyzing conversations and trends related to your brand → what people think about your brand, informs marketing and product development decisions
	- **Engagement**: how your audience interacts with your brand on social media (liking, sharing) + how you interact with your audience (e.g. thank a customer who loves your product; tell a customer with an issue how you will address it)
4. **Analytics and reporting**:
	- **Social media analytics**: the process of collecting data from your social media platforms and analyzing that data to make business decisions (e.g. positive mentions in a month, reach on different platforms month to month)
	- **Social media report**: a document that presents relevant data and analysis about your social media activities → inform stakeholders; monitor metrics such as followers, comments, clicks
5. **Paid social media**: better control and targeting than organic, reach a wider audience, grow your brand and broaden your presence → data from organic approaches informs the paid social strategy
#### Earned, owned, and paid social media
- Using all three types of digital media → build brand awareness, vary how the audience engages with your brand, increase sales
- **Earned media**: a personal or public promotion of a brand or product → marketing generated by your **customers** (telling a friend, posting on social media or a blog, writing a review)
	- Sources of earned social media: customer reviews and testimonials, mentions and comments, shares/retweets/likes, content about your business on third-party sites, blog posts, user-generated content
	- **User-generated content (UGC)**: any content created by people rather than brands (text, videos, images, reviews, ...)
		- E.g. a sock company: customers post pictures of themselves wearing the company's socks → UGC and earned media
	- Advantage: it's organic → **Organic social media**: any social media activity that does not require a paid promotion → the foundation of your social media strategy
- **Owned media**: all the digital content a brand fully controls (websites are one of the most common forms)
	- Sources of owned social media: blog sites, the brand's social media profiles (Facebook, Instagram), community forums
	- Benefits: **control the conversation** surrounding your brand (what to publish, how often, how users interact); market specific products or services; compelling owned media generates more earned media
- **Paid media**: any form of digital promotion a brand pays to put online (the fifth pillar)
	- Examples: image ads, video ads, story ads, influencer marketing
	- Reach customers who are not actively searching for your brand; target specific groups; promote content to drive more earned media and traffic to owned media
- Implemented together → improve brand awareness, generate leads, increase sales
#### The social media marketing funnel
- **Marketing funnel**: a graphic representation of the process through which people go from learning about a brand to becoming a loyal customer (→ [Course 2: marketing funnel](/blog/attract-and-engage-customers-with-digital-marketing/))
	- **Awareness**: first encounter with the brand; **Consideration**: thinks about doing business, actively searches for more information; **Conversion**: decides to make a purchase; **Loyalty**: decides to purchase again
	- Approach depends on the marketing channel (print, SEO, email use different tactics that support one another)
- **Awareness stage**: capture your audience's attention, get people to remember your brand
	- Target **key phrases** your audience searches for → create guides, videos, blogs around those topics
		- E.g. a career guidance company notices searches for "interview tips", "how to interview" → a guide with interview advice or a brief video tutorial
	- Keep it brief and simple; use **paid ads** targeting a specific audience or interest group
- **Consideration stage**: potential customers look for reviews, compare you with competitors → provide more detailed information to set you apart
	- Content: case studies, customer testimonials, product reviews, in-depth product tutorials → build trust and confidence
	- **Rule of seven**: a potential customer must see a message at least **seven times** before they're ready to take action → each appearance in someone's feed builds familiarity
- **Conversion stage**: capitalize on interest already shown (signed up for the email list, clicked an ad)
	- **Remarketing** (also **retargeting**): using paid ads to target customers who have visited your website, app, or social media profile
	- Incentives: discounts, free shipping
- **Loyalty stage**: it costs **five times** as much to attract a new customer than to retain existing ones
	- Remarketing ads to existing customers → repeat purchases
	- Make customers feel part of your **brand community**: content related to their purchase (e.g. prompt career-guidance customers to share on your community forum once they land a job)

## Module 2: Social media strategy, planning, and publishing
### Determine your social media marketing strategy
#### Welcome to module 2
- A clear strategy and careful planning → focus on the efforts that best serve your goals
- Module 2 covers the first two pillars:
	- **Strategy**: define goals, identify target audience, choose platforms, build brand identity on social media, select content types and formats that reinforce your brand
	- **Planning and publishing**: frequency and timing of posts, **real-time marketing**, social media calendar, social media management tools
#### Define the goals of your social media strategy
- **Social media strategy**: a guide to what you're trying to accomplish, how you'll accomplish it, and how you'll know it's accomplished → first step: **setting goals**
- Why set goals:
	- Goals **hold you accountable**
	- Goals **guide your budget** (define actions needed and the cost of each)
	- Goals help **measure your success** (analyze data, identify areas for improvement)
- Common social media marketing goals: building brand awareness, managing brand reputation, sales and lead generation, increasing community engagement, increasing web traffic, gaining customer and competitive insights
	- Social media goals should **align with the company's overall marketing objectives** → prove the value of your work, gain stakeholder support
- **SMART** goals: **Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound**
	- **Specific**: exactly what you want to achieve ("improve brand awareness" is too broad → followers or likes/shares/mentions? which platform?)
	- **Measurable**: can objectively determine it has been met (how much, how many, how will I know) → choose metrics (e.g. increase Instagram followers by **15%** over last quarter)
	- **Attainable**: realistic given constraints (time, budget, resources), but a little challenging to encourage growth
	- **Relevant**: lines up with the company's other marketing goals, priorities, values (e.g. to attract a younger demographic → Instagram, whose largest audience is aged **25–34**)
	- **Time-bound**: has a deadline → plan your schedule and set milestones
	- Final SMART goal: *By the end of the quarter, increase followers on Instagram by 15% over last quarter*
#### Identify your social media target audience
- **Social media target audience**: the specific group of people you want your company to reach on social media platforms → people most likely interested in your content, products, services; people whose problems you want to solve
	- Guides what, where, and when you share content; where money and time are best spent
- Step 1: compile information about **current customers and followers** (also from your research department, analysts, other marketers):
	- **Age**: decade of life, generation, stage of life (teens, young adults, parents, retirees)
	- **Location**: which geographical area to target, when to schedule posts and ads
	- **Language**: which languages to use (some speak a different language at home)
	- **Interests**: what they like to do, what excites them, what they care about
	- **Needs**: problems your product or service could help solve
- Step 2: assess how people **engage with your brand** on social media: what they say, like and dislike, why they follow you, what else they talk about online → **social listening tools**
- Step 3: research which **platforms** they use → reach them where they're most active (no point posting regularly on Pinterest if your audience is mostly on LinkedIn)
- Step 4: observe the **competition**: who they target, how they reach out, what they do well and not so well, how often they post, which content formats they use
- Marketing to a highly specific audience → content that speaks to them → earn loyalty
#### Popular social media platforms
- Choose the platforms your **target audiences** use; deliver content users on each platform prefer while staying authentic to your **brand voice**
- **Monthly active users**: the number of unique customers who visit a platform over a month-long span → can change dramatically; affects where you place priorities
- **Social networking** (Facebook, LinkedIn): allow users to interact with each other, for personal and professional reasons
	- **Facebook**: small businesses keep in touch and share updates (new products or services, sales, hours of operation)
	- **LinkedIn**: manage professional identity, network; companies share updates and projects, engage with and hire professionals
	- Pro tip: these platforms encourage various content types (visuals, text, external links) → share different types each day
- **Microblogging/Blogging**: **blogging** = self-published writing that lives online; **microblogging** = blogging on a smaller scale, short-form content distributed quickly and frequently (X, Reddit)
	- **X**: optimize engagement and interaction; search function; brands use the "trending" section strategically
	- **Reddit**: web chatter and sharing to create a sense of community; **subreddits** = niche online communities on a certain topic (TV shows, hobbies, political or religious affiliations, industries)
- **Photo sharing** (Instagram, Pinterest): visually appealing feeds; engage your community and new users
	- **Instagram**: photo and video sharing; brands can link products directly in photos → purchases in the app (shoppable content)
	- **Pinterest**: brands can link directly to websites or landing pages in captions and replies; users save posts to "boards" (e.g. an interior design company posts mockups linking to its booking website)
	- Pro tip: check analytics on what content your audience **saves** → keep delivering tailored content
- **Video sharing** (YouTube, TikTok, Vimeo, Instagram Live, Facebook Live): upload, share, live stream; informational and community-building
	- **YouTube, Vimeo**: long-form video (e.g. a fitness instructor leading a full class, a carpenter showing woodworking) → most time-consuming and specialized content type, not right for everyone
	- **TikTok**: short-form video; how-to videos, behind-the-scenes, community-building (songs, dances)
	- **Instagram Live, Facebook Live**: live content; often question-and-answer style videos
- Popular social media platforms: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/sDVon
#### Set up a professional social media profile
- Create **separate accounts** for personal and professional use (personal accounts may contain content you don't want to share with professional contacts) → example: an **X** profile for recruiters, hiring managers, professional contacts
- **Open an account** at X.com (Google/Apple account, phone, or email; name, birth date, country; verification codes; unique password)
- **Profile picture**: first impression → professional, high-quality photo clearly showing your face; no suggestive, humorous, or group photos; professional or neutral attire (solid colors); looks like you regularly
- **Header**: banner image behind the profile image → a solid color or an image representing you as a professional; doesn't distract from the profile picture (free public-domain images, e.g. Wikimedia Commons)
- **Handle**: like a username, follows an "@" → ideally matches your actual name (e.g. @JaneSmith); professional and memorable; avoid numbers, use underscores (@Jane_Smith); can include business or industry name
- **Bio**: introduce yourself in **280** or fewer characters (schools, jobs, internships, this certificate); bios are searchable → include **keywords** so recruiters find you
- **Location**: helps recruiters searching for candidates in your area
- **Links**: website, blog, portfolio, other social media accounts → more of your work, more traffic
- **Explore, follow, and post**:
	- **#Explore**: curated hashtags, subjects, news
	- Follow topics related to your professional interests ("Business and Finance", "Careers"); follow people you admire and the people they follow
	- Post regularly on career-related content (breaking news, industry news, GIFs and memes, infographics, images, videos, trending topics); keep posts professional, avoid controversial topics (politics, religion); join relevant conversations
- Key takeaways: same concepts apply to other platforms (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram); add links to professional accounts on your resume; stay informed about industry trends; make contacts that could lead to career opportunities
- Set up a professional social media profile: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/WXZps
#### Choose social media platforms for your campaign
- Reaching your audience = right content + right time + **right platform**
	- Right platform → reach your target audience, drive more website traffic; spend time and money where results are most likely
- Over **100** social media platforms worldwide → strategies to choose:
	- **Learn the platforms** and how people communicate on them (e.g. Twitter: real-time information, **280**-character limit → breaking news, brief content, real-time interaction)
	- **Target audience**: where are they most active? (Facebook may have the most active users, but for an audience under **25** → platforms with a younger demographic like TikTok)
	- **Campaign goals** aligned with business goals (brand awareness, leads, community engagement) → e.g. **LinkedIn** (resumes, job search, sharing expertise, networking) to increase **brand authority**
	- **Your company**: products and services, brand identity, business type (an art distributor → Instagram; a company producing educational videos → YouTube)
	- **Competitors**: where they post and get the most engagement → you'll likely perform (or struggle) similarly on those channels
	- **Available resources**: time, money, energy → how many people to manage platforms, time per platform, advertising budget
#### Build your brand identity on social media
- Refresher: **SMART** social media goals; **customer personas** (naming them and associating a photo is common practice) (→ [Course 2: customer personas](/blog/attract-and-engage-customers-with-digital-marketing/)); **prioritize your platforms**
- **Brand position statement**: outlines exactly what your company does and for whom, and what makes you different from competitors → internally guides the team; externally tells customers about your brand and goals
	- Need: audience, customer challenges or needs, company name, products/services or **differentiator** (what sets you apart), mission statement
	- Template: *For [audience] who [customer challenge/need], [Company Name] provides [product and differentiator] because [brand promise or mission].*
	- Scenario **EveryWay EveryWear** (comfortable professional clothing for people who work long hours giving presentations, pitches, sales): *For [working professionals] who need a [stylish, professional, and functional wardrobe], [EveryWay EveryWear] provides [plush, comfortable clothes to wear in the workplace]—because [when you're working long hours, comfort should come first].*
- **Designing brand identity**: consistent **color palette, logos, digital assets** across social pages; follow existing **brand guidelines** (colors, sizes, fonts, logos) or create some
	- **Brand voice**: the distinct personality a brand takes on in its communications
	- Tips:
		- **Standardize your brand's colors** across pages: same color palettes and filters for every image
		- **Team up with influencers** that fit your brand identity → brand awareness, consideration, conversions; collaborating signals your brands align
		- **Create a "voice"** and stick to it (switching from humorous to moody on the same day is off-putting); recognizable in captions, graphics, video, photos
- Build your brand identity on social media: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/TD9e1
### Offer content variety
#### Types of content on social media
- Effective content generates engagement → a content plan with a **variety of content types**
- **Content buckets**: categories to group your marketing content; broad themes used to achieve a particular goal or make customers feel a certain way
	- **Entertaining**: for people on the platform to enjoy themselves → helps the audience relate to you (viral videos, jokes, comics, contests or giveaways, memes)
		- **Meme**: an amusing or interesting item (caption, picture, video) spread widely online, especially through social media
	- **Educational**: builds trust, positions you as an industry leader (video trainings, infographics, industry research, case studies, FAQs, tips, how-to posts)
	- **Inspirational**: often features real people using your products → authentic, reinforces message, values, vision (case studies, testimonials and quotes, amazing facts and trivia, personal stories)
	- **Conversational**: encourages engagement → suggest a topic or viewpoint and invite dialogue; ask a question, poll, fill in the blank, ask for advice
	- **Promotional**: markets products/services to current customers and draws new followers (coupons, discounts, free gifts for sign-ups)
- **Content formats**: images, videos, written posts, stories, GIFs, links, polls, live videos
	- **Videos**: grab attention, easier to consume than text, more engaging than pictures (how-to guides, video tours, product demonstrations) → drive traffic and sales
	- **Images**: the most frequently posted content type; absorbed instantly, immediate impression of your brand (infographics, animated GIFs, captioned photos, screenshots)
	- **Written posts** (articles, blogs, guides): information about products, services, company; start conversations
	- **Stories**: images and short videos that last **1–7 days** before disappearing; stickers, filters, sound; update the audience on events, offers, announcements
	- **Live videos**: broadcast conversations, performances, Q&As, webinars, virtual events → instant questions, comments, reactions; sense of community
- Key: choose the **format that best matches your content bucket**
#### Anna - Use earned, owned, and paid media in social media marketing campaigns
- Anna: Senior Program Manager at Google, **Google Developers Studio** → oversees social strategy for about **70** developer-facing accounts (Twitter, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, blogs)
	- Purpose (Developer Relations): make the life of a developer easier → keep people up to date on technology changes, upgrades, announcements; answer questions; provide resources
- **Earned media**: buzz that happens organically, "conversation out in the wild" → similar to **public relations** (influence a journalist or blogger to write about you)
	- Get your product to the right **influencer** → organic talk creates buzz and awareness among new audiences
	- E.g. developers watching event content and having conversations about it = natural buzz
- **Owned media**: you own the channels (Twitter, Facebook, Instagram) → be as creative as possible, **control the narrative** for your personal brand or business
- **Paid media**: guaranteed to hit a specific audience you pay for → on Facebook or Instagram, a small budget can make a big impact (especially for a local business)
	- Use owned/organic channels to **test messaging and creative assets** → when content gains traction, **boost** it with paid media
- Once you've found your niche and own a channel, mixing earned, owned, and paid on that channel gets you much further than a single tactic
- Advice: just do it; your own channels reflect your brand → hiring managers look at your social media (content decisions, eye for photography and creative)
#### How to acquire earned media
- **Earned media**: any positive digital exposure generated through personal or public recommendations → word-of-mouth from friends, family, unpaid influencer posts, product reviews
	- Costs nothing and shows your audience believes in you, but probably the **most difficult** tactic since you have no control over it
	- Examples: customer testimonials and reviews, blog posts about your product, shares and reposts, mentions on social pages
- Effective earned media strategy:
	- **Create engaging content** (most important) → more likely to be shared
	- **Nurture relationships** with other brands, media members, journalists → positive press
	- Provide **more than satisfactory customer service** → customers share your brand
	- **Encourage people in your organization to share**
- How to acquire earned media: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/eypUv
#### Case study: How Name Glo acquires earned media
- **Name Glo**: New York City neon light studio specializing in custom designs (B2C and B2B); mission: light up people's lives with personalized neon lights (names, business names, mantras, catchy phrases); founders **Lena Imamura** and **Sas Simon** credit part of their success to sharing designs on **Instagram**
- **Challenge**: small business with limited time and resources; founders manage social media themselves; all content and growth is **organic** (no sponsored posts or ads) → entire strategy is to acquire earned media
- **Approach: create a post-worthy product**
	- Include customers in every stage (ideating, planning, creating) → an **experience**, not just a product → customers feel invested
	- A unique, high-quality product customers want to photograph and post → customers' photos are often high quality, so no need to stage photos (saves time and money)
	- **Repost** client photos as Instagram stories and usually in the feed

### Plan when to publish
#### Determine the frequency and timing of posts
- A consistent presence lets your brand be seen and discovered, but **more is not always better** → stay in the audience's mind without overwhelming their feeds
- **Frequency** depends on how each platform works:
	- **Chronological feeds** (e.g. Twitter's option to show the latest content first) → posting multiple times a day reaches different time zones and points in the day → aim for at least several times a day
	- **Social media algorithms**: a way of sorting posts in a user's feed based on relevancy rather than the order in which they were published → **algorithm-based** platforms (Facebook, Instagram): publish quality content less frequently (too many posts → drop in engagement); once a day or several times a week depending on the brand
	- General guidelines only → trial and error, testing, analyzing results, available resources
- **Timing** (optimal time varies by platform):
	- Generally effective: **first thing in the morning** (catching up on feeds), **around lunchtime** (gap in schedules), **just after working hours** (finding out what they missed)
	- Find the best times for your brand:
		- When your **target audience is active online** (consider their **time zone**)
		- **Patterns in your best-performing posts** (times of day, days of the week) → analytics tools
		- **Seasons and holidays** (e.g. consumers more likely to book a vacation in **January** → travel brands post appealing destination images)
		- **Current events** (e.g. national disaster or conflict → unschedule posts to be sensitive, or add a post to show support)
#### Real-time marketing
- The social media news cycle is fast-paced → a story breaks on Twitter and the discussion may end in an instant
- **Real-time marketing (RTM)**: marketing that is happening in real time → often on social media; brands join conversations about big stories to boost engagement and position their brand in discussions
- Effective RTM content must be:
	- **Relevant**: what is happening right now?
	- **Clever**: shows your brand's personality and wit
	- **Fresh**: a topic that trended Wednesday may be stale by Thursday
	- **On-target**: of interest to your audience
	- **Attention-grabbing**: makes people stop scrolling and engage immediately
- RTM strategies:
	- **Always be listening**: keep communication open with the social team (usually first to know about trends); be present on social media as often as possible
	- **Use your brand's resources**: design and copyediting teams for quick, clever, high-quality posts
	- **Leverage social media influencers** to repost or interact with RTM assets
	- **Know where the conversation is happening** (a moment on one platform may not be discussed on others)
	- **Promote some of your posts**: algorithms may not show organic posts to enough people in time → **promoted post**: a social media post that you pay the platform to make more visible
- Users become desensitized to trends quickly → capitalize on the moments with RTM

### Social listening
#### Welcome to module 3
- Scenario: a product has a small defect and customers complain on social media → if you don't notice and address it, the company's reputation could be jeopardized → listening and engaging keeps the reputation intact
- Third pillar: **listening and engagement**
	- **Social listening**: track and analyze conversations and trends about your brand → gain insights; social listening tools
	- **Engagement**: communicate with customers, respond to different types of communications, develop ongoing relationships
	- Write and design engaging content, develop your **brand voice** on different platforms, **repurpose** existing content to save time and resources
#### The importance of social listening
- **Social listening**: tracking and analyzing conversations and trends related to your brand → understand what people think, inform marketing and product development decisions
- Two-step process:
	1. **Monitor** social media platforms for mentions of your brand, products, services, competitors, and related keywords
	2. **Analyze** the information and **plan actions** (from responding to a dissatisfied customer to adjusting your entire strategy)
- Why it matters:
	- Understand **social media sentiment**: the attitude and feelings people have about your brand on social media → what's working and where to make changes
	- Opportunity to **engage** with customers and followers (comments of love, questions) → customers want to feel heard → brand loyalty, higher **customer retention** rates
	- **Address negative sentiment**: pinpoint the cause, find pain points, offer solutions
		- E.g. customers mention a problem ordering from your app → escalate to the IT department, post that you're addressing their concerns
		- Be mindful of responding to individuals directly vs. posting publicly (an issue affecting a small group may get more attention if posted publicly)
	- **Analyze the competition**: what competitors do and how their customers feel (unhappy with a product → improve a similar product; their content outperforms yours → analyze why it resonates with your shared audience)
	- **Increase customer acquisition**: followers are also people interested in your content → discover content they enjoy and create content that draws them into your brand
#### Social listening strategies
- **Identify keywords** that help you find conversations about your brand (vs. SEO keyword research, where you put keywords customers search for on your webpage → [Course 2: SEO](/blog/attract-and-engage-customers-with-digital-marketing/)):
	- Brand and product names (including variations and common misspellings), slogans, names of key people in the company, industry buzzwords, hashtags related to the brand (yours and competitors')
	- **Hashtag**: a word or phrase preceded by the pound symbol that indicates a piece of content relates to a specific topic or category → a digital label that helps people find content (e.g. a coffee company: #coffeetime, #coffeelover, #bestcoffee, #coffeedrinker)
- **Determine where to listen**: people may talk differently on LinkedIn than on Facebook, or mention you more on Twitter than Instagram → listen on each platform
- **Find out what people are saying**: what customers like most, most common issues, most frequently asked questions, associations with your brand, what content they share → understand what customers truly need instead of acting on assumptions
- **Assess your competition**: which of their content performs better and how to improve yours; which of their products customers are dissatisfied with; what new products or solutions they offer
	- Learn from their **mistakes** (e.g. content that receives a lot of negative responses → avoid similar content) → save time, energy, resources
- **Gain industry insights**: gaps a new product could solve, major events or issues relevant to your brand, frequently asked questions to create content around
- **Social listening tool**: software that helps you track mentions of your brand, relevant keywords, and direct feedback from multiple social media platforms in one place
#### Case study: How EatMoveRest improves content with social listening
- **EatMoveRest**: healthy lifestyle brand from Omaha, Nebraska, founded by **Erin and Dusty Stanczyk** → people eat, move, and rest every day, so do them as best as possible; vegan meal planning, plant-based recipes, expert advice, coaching, tips for sustainable living; a global online community of like-minded followers
- **Challenges** (small business: too much feedback, not enough time to analyze it):
	- **Large volume of user comments** (YouTube and Instagram comments are endless)
	- **No barriers or limits on expression** → hard to find useful feedback among negative or hateful comments
	- **Inability to reply to or address every comment**
- **Approach** (best practices):
	- **Identify which channel is a priority** for reviewing comments → prioritized the **YouTube channel** (subscribers' opinions matter most; replying makes them feel part of the community)
	- **Offer designated times and methods for feedback** → set aside time to address collected feedback, focus on constructive comments, ignore noisy ones
		- Instagram **Q&A feature**: a submission box below posted content → direct, relevant feedback on products, apps, website, recipes, content in one organized location; prompt replies
- **Results**:
	- **Reversing changes to content**: "a day in the life of the Stanczyk family" videos (vibrant colors, uplifting music, positive reinforcement) → subscribers preferred uplifting music over a darker track; a video about animal agriculture got thumbs down and no shares → they now avoid that kind of content on YouTube
	- **Improving the meal planning app**: Instagram Q&A feedback that recipes were old → users expected weekly rollover → recipes now updated more frequently
- Conclusion: actively listen to understand how users interact with your brand; even a brand with loyal followers benefits from changes based on constructive feedback

### Engagement on social media
#### Social media engagement
- **Social media engagement**: measures how people are interacting with your social media accounts and content → actions such as likes, favorites, comments, shares, retweets, saves, clicks, hashtags, mentions → does your content resonate?
- **Connect** with your audience: per a **Sprout Social** report, when customers feel connected to a brand, more than **half** increase their spending on it and more than **three-quarters** keep buying from it rather than a competitor
- Ways to increase engagement:
	- **Be social**: communicate frequently, like the host of a party (not always the center of attention, but everyone has a good time) (→ [Course 6: Social presence across seasons](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
	- **Respond to comments and mentions** → two-way communication, healthy relationships
	- **Excellent customer service**: answer questions, provide resources, show empathy
	- **Respond quickly** → customers feel heard; increased satisfaction and trust
	- **Authentic personal voice**: sign off on posts, address customers by first name, respond with humor or warmth, show images or videos of employees
	- **Know your audience**: language, tone, images differ (a video game company vs. a quilting supply company); create **valuable content** (teach, inspire, provide resources) → credible source, builds trust; help them imagine using your product; not every post has to be about your products
	- **Get creative**: contests, polls, quizzes, questions, spotlighting customers and employees, eliciting user-generated content, coupons and discounts, giveaways in exchange for followers, likes, retweets, tags

#### Respond to social media users
- Why respond: replying to comments, messages, mentions, and tags makes users feel heard, makes you appear accessible → shows your brand cares about the customer experience

### Composing engaging content
#### Write for social media
- Strategies to increase engagement won't help if content isn't compelling; companies create content in-house or outsource to freelancers or media agencies → either way you're responsible for content that reflects well on your brand
- Best practices:
	- **Write to your audience**: research interests, values, needs, obstacles → put their needs at the center; describe how your product improves their life; write as if speaking directly to each person
	- **Speak like your audience**: use similar language to theirs
	- **Tailor writing to each platform** (professionals on LinkedIn vs. young adults on Facebook); intentionally unexpected language can grab attention
	- **Keep posts short and simple**: avoid jargon; headlines, bullets, lists; short sentences and paragraphs; bold important information → easy to scan
	- **Include a call to action**: **call to action (CTA)** = an instruction that tells the customer what to do next (also used in display ads, email marketing)
		- On social media: like or share a post, answer a question, take a quiz, read another piece of content, go to your website, subscribe to your newsletter, connect on another platform
	- **Write in your brand voice**: **brand voice** = the distinct personality a brand takes on in its communications (language and tone; e.g. casual and conversational, or professional and serious) → consistent across all channels → recognition and familiarity
	- **Check grammar and spelling** (errors reflect poorly even with a casual voice)
#### Develop your brand voice on social media
- **Brand voice**: how the attitudes and values of a brand are reflected in all of its content (→ [Course 6: Brand voice in a Shopify store](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
- Why it matters:
	- **Stand out** in a crowded space, distinguish you from competitors
	- **More recognizable** → familiarity, brand awareness
	- **Consistency** in language and messaging → trust and loyalty
- How to define it:
	- Describe your **brand's personality**: if your brand were a person, what would they say, how would they act, what relationship to the customer (coach, friend, parent)? Adjectives (friendly, upbeat, helpful; smart, confident, formal; casual, sarcastic, bold)
	- **Review current and past content**: how your audience speaks and interacts with you, what resonated, what language they use
	- Be **honest and authentic**: avoid complicated terminology, acronyms, jargon; speak naturally, as in person → audiences want to speak to a real person
- **Tone**: how your brand's voice is applied → varies by audience, situation, platform (upbeat and excited for a product launch; serious and empathetic to support a community after a natural disaster) → one brand voice, many tones
- **Brand voice guidelines**: describe how your brand should be presented in writing → mission and values, personality, target audience, common vocabulary and phrases, tone in different situations or platforms, examples → reference for writing and reviewing; consistency across contributors

#### Make social media posts accessible
- Over **1 billion** people have a disability (more than the populations of the US, Canada, France, Italy, Japan, Mexico, and Brazil combined) → can affect vision, movement, thinking, memory, learning, communication, hearing, mental health, social relationships
- **Accessibility**: considering the needs of people with disabilities when products, services, and facilities are built or modified, making them usable by people of all abilities (→ [Course 5: Make presentations accessible](/blog/assess-for-success-marketing-analytics-and-measurement/))
- Guidelines:
	- **Video captioning**: helps people with hearing loss, non-native speakers, people who can't play sound (quiet or crowded places)
		- **Closed captions**: overlaid on video, can be turned on and off
		- **Open captions**: embedded directly in video, can't be hidden
		- Check which type each platform supports
	- **Alternative text (alt text)**: a brief, written description of an image with the primary purpose of assisting individuals who are visually impaired; some platforms auto-generate it with AI (edit it to save time)
		- Don't include "image" (screen readers already announce it): "cup of coffee with foam in the shape of a heart on top"
		- Focus on the **purpose** of the image, not every detail (a clock illustrating a limited-time offer → no need to mention the time 10:10)
	- **Color contrast**: ratio (L1/L2) of the luminescence of the lighter color to the darker color, from **1:1** (white on white) to **21:1** (black on white); red on white 4:1, green on white 1.4:1, blue on white 8.6:1
		- Use at least **4.5:1** for text and background (e.g. blue on white rather than red or green); WebAIM contrast checker (hex color codes)
	- **Fonts**: custom fonts, italics, and other styles can be hard for screen readers
	- **Hashtags**: use **CamelCase** (#VirtualClassroom, not #virtualclassroom) → screen readers read separate words correctly
	- **Emojis**: use sparingly (screen readers describe each one in several words, e.g. "Grinning Face with Big Eyes")
	- **Language**: avoid terms that undervalue or dehumanize people ("blind people" or "partially-sighted people" instead of "the blind"); rephrase common statements ("enjoy the outdoors" instead of "take a walk")
- More accessible posts → wider audience, greater interest

#### Understand social media analytics
- Social listening gathers **qualitative data**: data describing qualities or characteristics, gathered through observation (e.g. language people use about your business)
- Analytics looks at **quantitative data**: information that can be counted or compared on a numeric scale (e.g. number of clicks in a time period) (→ [Data Science: quantitative and qualitative data](/blog/prepare-data-for-exploration/))
- **Social media analytics**: the process of tracking, collecting, and analyzing data from social media platforms to improve an organization's strategic business decisions (e.g. what percentage of followers interacted with a post, how many people watched your videos)
- How analytics supports campaigns:
	- **Understand your audience**: which content gets the best engagement; when your audience is most active → best time to post
	- **Inform platform choices**: measure engagement, traffic, sales per platform → prioritize the best-performing platforms
	- **Learn what content drives results**: images vs. videos vs. links per platform, which types of images perform best (Facebook and Twitter analytics show top-engaging content types)
	- **Learn from competitors**: e.g. Twitter lets you analyze all public conversations → what they do well and their mistakes
	- **Measure social media ROI** (return on investment) for clients (e.g. brand awareness goal → followers gained per platform in a period) → prove effectiveness to stakeholders
	- **Improve your strategy**: focus time, effort, budget; compile demographic information about your target audience
- Tools: platforms' own analytics + cross-platform tools such as **Google Analytics**, **Sprout Social**, **Hootsuite Analytics**, **Buffer Analyze**
#### Metrics to track with social media analytics
- **Metric**: a quantifiable measurement that is used to track and assess a business objective → your social media goal (from campaign and business goals) determines which metrics to focus on
- Metrics by stage of the social media marketing funnel:
	- **Awareness** → **brand awareness** metrics: attention your brand receives across platforms during a reporting period (week, month, quarter)
		- Shares, mentions, links to your content, **impressions** (an **impression** is when a piece of content is displayed to your target audience)
		- **Potential reach**: how many people have potentially seen a post since you published it (e.g. a Retweet → a certain percentage of that person's followers may see it)
	- **Consideration** → engagement metrics:
		- **Applause rate**: the number of approval actions (likes, mentions, Retweets, favorites) a post receives relative to your total number of followers → high = audience finds the content valuable, interest that could lead to a purchase; guides what content to create next
	- **Conversion**:
		- **Referrals**: how someone was guided to your website (from all platforms or specific ones)
		- **Conversions**: the completion of an activity that contributes to the success of a business (clicking a link, buying something)
		- **Social conversions**: actions a person takes on your website because of having recently seen your social media campaigns
	- **Loyalty**:
		- **Customer testimonials**: any customer review, assessment, comment, endorsement, or interview relating to your brand → trust, credibility, social media presence
		- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**: predicts future customer engagement by asking "How likely is it that you would recommend our product to a friend?" on a scale of 1 to 10 → unhappy customers, satisfied but unenthusiastic customers, or loyal enthusiasts → measure satisfaction, predict future sales
- Metrics show whether your strategy is successful over time; add metrics relevant to your business as the strategy evolves

#### Social testing
- **Social testing**: an experimental process that shows how social media content is performing among target audiences → data-driven insights on which content is effective, which post types get the most engagement, how to allocate budget
	- Type of test depends on your business goals
- **A/B testing**: compares two versions of a post with a **single** differing variable
	- E.g. two paid ads sending users to your homepage, identical except one headline has a popular song reference → if it gets more clicks, use pop culture references with more confidence
- **Multivariate testing**: compares two or more versions of a post with **several** differing variables
	- E.g. four versions: (1) image + caption; (2) GIF + different caption; (3) different copy + different color scheme; (4) changed color scheme + the GIF from version 2
- **Results**: evaluate correctly, draw conclusions based on statistical data; run tests several times (a week, a month, or longer) → more tests, more certainty; choose variables thoughtfully
- You only know what you know → identify a specific goal before testing; don't be afraid to try new things
- Social testing: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/AL5kY
#### Use social media data to drive marketing strategy
- Adapt your strategy during campaigns based on analytics → after identifying metrics, choosing a tool, and collecting data, determine what the data means and which strategic decisions to make
- How data can affect decisions:
	- **Improve your content**: engagement data shows what resonates; effect of post length, visuals, hashtags, tone (e.g. infographic posts get higher engagement → publish more infographics)
	- **Track competitors' engagement**: their best-performing content, platforms where it's shared most, most effective influencers → adjust your strategy
	- **Spot emerging trends** via shares and Retweets (e.g. **augmented reality**: a technology that superimposes a computer-generated image onto a user's view of the real world → interactive, highly effective for engagement) → real-time marketing content (e.g. your audience shares a particular news story → create related content)
	- **Determine the best platforms** (e.g. posts perform much better on Facebook than Instagram → keep similar content on Facebook, adjust the Instagram strategy)
	- **Customize content** by customer persona (e.g. local content referencing local issues, interests, locations, weather) → personalized content seems authentic and trustworthy → drives engagement and conversions

### The value of paid ads on social media
#### Welcome to module 5
- Campaigns usually combine **organic and paid media**; organic alone may not achieve all goals → fifth pillar: **paid social media**
- Module 5 covers: benefits of paid social media; using organic and paid together; **ad formats** and choosing them by goal; **remarketing** to people who already showed interest; setting a paid social media **budget** and ad costs
#### Benefits of paid social media
- **Paid social media**: displaying paid ads or sponsored marketing messages on social media platforms to target a specific audience → highly relevant ads or **boosting** organic content to reach new and returning customers
	- Examples:
		- **Pay-per-click (PPC) advertising**: you pay each time someone clicks on your ad
		- **Branded content**: any post produced by a third-party product, brand, or sponsor
		- **Influencer-generated content**: any post created by an influencer that promotes a brand's products and services
	- Social media ads are identified by a **"sponsored"** or **"promoted"** tag
- Additional benefits beyond organic:
	- **Increase brand awareness**: prominent position in feeds, reach people not searching for or following you
	- **Reach your audience quickly**: organic posts can take days to be seen due to algorithms (costly for short campaigns) → paid increases rank in feeds
	- **Target your most relevant customers**: age range, region, followers, previous customers, people who **bounced** from your website; users' **ad preferences** → ads shown to people most likely to click
	- **Increase remarketing capabilities**: **remarketing** = displaying paid ads to customers who have visited your website, app, or social media profile → multiple times, different formats → more likely to buy from you over competitors (e.g. a shirt they viewed on your website)
#### Integrate paid social media into your strategy
- Organic and paid work together: both can gain customers and followers, promote the brand, research audiences
	- **Organic**: ideal for nurturing connections through communication and relationship-building
	- **Paid**: best for **raising brand awareness, targeting specific audiences, driving conversions**
- Tactics:
	- **Boost top-performing organic content** identified via analytics (e.g. Hootsuite's **promote** feature, or automatic triggers based on criteria) → Cindy promoted her video on a human-centric approach to marketing to scale its impact
	- **Lookalike audiences**: people with similar demographics and behaviors to your existing customers who haven't yet been introduced to your brand (e.g. similar to customers who purchased in the past six months) → highly relevant ads
	- **A/B test ads** before spending a big chunk of budget: compare two versions of an ad to see which has the higher **conversion rate**; test with a small audience → messaging, calls to action, copywriting, visuals, placement, formats, demographic groups (→ [Course 2: A/B testing](/blog/attract-and-engage-customers-with-digital-marketing/))
		- A/B testing organic posts also shows what to promote and what elements to include in ads
	- **Remarketing** to drive conversions (e.g. someone adds jeans to their cart but doesn't buy → a remarketing ad for those jeans or similar items in their feed)
### Target your social advertising
#### Develop a paid social media strategy
- Steps to set up an ad campaign (example: Twitter; most platforms are similar):
	1. **Start with your objective**: more followers, more engagement, brand awareness, sign-ups or purchases from an offer → guides users to your end goal (website offer, email subscription form, social profile); ensures you track the right metrics and KPIs → select the objective when setting up the ad
	2. **Determine your audience**: build around one of your **customer personas** (a group of similar people in a desirable audience); reaching a wide audience with ads gets expensive → narrow the audience with filters: gender, age, physical location, keywords, interests, events
	3. **Choose platforms**: ones you're familiar with and have an established audience on; for a first campaign, the platform that is your biggest source of leads
	4. **Define the campaign length**: one week, two weeks, a month → specific start and end dates
	5. **Set a budget**: research average advertising cost on the platform; some require a **minimum ad budget** per ad group; spending more can unlock special features and work with platform managers
		- Ads are paid through an **auction-style format** (bidding for an optimal ad position) → if you win, pay the agreed amount per click, view, like, or impression
	6. **Develop and upload creatives**: **creatives** = any content that can be promoted in a campaign (text, images, GIFs, videos) → follow **ad specs** (dimensions, sizes, formats); craft calls to action and messaging; organic testing to optimize assets
	7. **Launch the campaign** after the platform approves the ad → posts automatically at the start date and time; plan with a social media calendar; fine-tune during initial stages
#### Ad formats on social media
- Best practice: choose ad formats based on **advertising goals**:

| Goal | Best format | Advantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximize awareness | Video | Transfer maximum amount of information in a short amount of time |
| Maximize clicks | Static image | Combine with a compelling call to action |
| Maximize conversions | Static image | Combine with a click-to-purchase call to action |
| Maximize app downloads | Video | Provide instruction on how to use the app |
| Maximize app downloads | Custom, platform-specific formats (if available) | Increase app installations with an ad format dedicated to a specific platform |
| Maximize engagement | Video | Tell stories that draw people in |

- Then choose platforms and look up their **specifications** for those formats (e.g. a static image ad on LinkedIn → consult the ads guide, then the specification) (→ [Course 6: Platforms for e-commerce ads](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
- Ad type also depends on **budget** (some ads are more expensive to run)
- Ad formats on social media: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/J8Tkz
#### Choose social media platforms for your ads
- Know where your target audience is; each platform has an intended use (socializing vs. networking and careers) → choosing poorly can damage your relationship with your audience; test which platforms your audience engages with ads on most
- **Facebook**: over **1.4 billion** average daily users → GIFs, videos, static images, carousels → website traffic, brand awareness, conversion rate
- **Instagram**: content must be **image-based**; paid influencer Reels with "#ad", Instagram Stories ads → effective for conversions
- **X**: for a more **niche** audience; community-based connection, timely interactions and updates; conversation and engagement make users feel connected to brands
- **YouTube**: video content for audiences aged **18–49** (reaches more 18–49-year-olds than every cable TV network); community-based, original video content, engagement in comments
- **LinkedIn**: for brands that cater to **other businesses (B2B)** → professionals and business leaders
- Social media changes constantly → be ready to pivot; base your ad strategy on **audience preferences** (gather data, cultivate relationships)
- Choose social media platforms for your ads: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/gJHWO
#### Remarketing on social media
- Example: you view a product on a website, leave without buying, then see an ad for it on social media → remarketing in practice
- **Remarketing** (or **retargeting**): displays paid ads to target customers who have visited your website, app, or social media profile → another chance to reach leads; keeps your brand in mind while they evaluate options; **personalized** experience → more conversions (→ [Course 6: Dynamic remarketing and cart abandonment](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
	- Used across channels: paid search, display, email, social media
- Two main types:
	- **Pixel-based remarketing**: sends ads automatically after placing a **cookie** in the user's browser that tracks pages and products viewed → ad networks (Google Ads, Facebook Ad Manager) show specific ads when they browse social media or other websites
		- **Cookie**: a small file stored on devices that allows you to track user behavior and analyze traffic
	- **List-based remarketing**: uses lists of existing customers or visitors who provided their email addresses → upload the email list to the platform; your audience sees your ads as they browse
		- Protect privacy: Google and Facebook **hash** uploaded data → **hashing**: a security method that turns personal information in your email lists into randomized code
- Best practices:
	- **Personalize ads by audience**: segment into interest-based groups → **custom audiences** (e.g. on Twitter): upload your own email lists to reach highly specific groups
	- **Remarket to past purchasers**: almost **half** of overall revenue of U.S. online retailers comes from repeat customers (e.g. bought a toy → offers on children's products)
	- **Limit frequency** so users don't feel overwhelmed
	- **Untag visitors who already purchased** so converted customers don't see too many ads
#### Create an effective call to action in your social ad
- **Call to action (CTA)**: an instruction that tells the customer what to do next → usually buttons, clickable images, or links
- Writing a CTA:
	- Start with your **brand goals** (e.g. more website traffic → a link to your homepage)
	- Give a **reason to click**: what will they get out of it?
	- **Clarity** first: users must understand what they're asked to do and how it benefits them (enthusiasm and wit are still good)
	- **Action words**: sign up, visit, click, download, shop, follow
	- **Urgency**: not to trick or exploit, but be clear if an offer expires ("today only")
	- **Brand voice**: the CTA reflects the tone of your captions and copy (warm and inviting, cool and succinct, casual and silly)
- Create an effective call to action in your social ad: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/mJz9c
### Managing a social advertising budget
#### Components of a paid social media budget
- Goals of the paid strategy (brand awareness, followers) guide the budget; as a newcomer you may not decide funding, but should know the components
- Budgets vary; generally set by **annual revenue**: per a **Gartner** survey, businesses spend around **10–13%** of annual revenue on marketing, about **25%** of that on paid media (search and social); more or less depending on past ROI
- Components:
	- **Ad spend**: how much your company spends directly on advertisements (paid to Twitter, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn to serve ads; also paid to influencers)
	- **Tools and technologies** to launch, manage, optimize campaigns: social media management software, social listening and analytics software, photo and video editing programs, stock photo and video subscriptions, **influencer marketing platforms** (software with influencer discovery tools such as large searchable databases of potential influencers)
		- Try free tools before paid ones; set aside budget for unexpected costs
	- **Content creation**: photography, graphic design, video development (talent and production costs), copywriting
	- **Management**: strategy, content planning and publishing, social listening, engagement, analytics and reporting (sometimes via a digital marketing agency; internal work may still have costs)
#### The cost of advertising on social media
- Most social ads are sold in an **auction**: set a maximum bid for a target result (a click, a number of impressions) or a maximum budget per day → the outcome determines what you pay
- **Bidding strategy**: tells how much you will pay for each user action related to an ad → depends on campaign goal, advertising budget, market research; varies by platform
	- **CPC (cost per click)**: the amount you pay when someone clicks on your ad → cost-effective (pay only on clicks); great for driving **website traffic**
	- **CPA (cost per action)**, also **cost per acquisition**: the amount you pay when someone completes a desired action after clicking (newsletter sign-up, watching a video, requesting a callback, purchase) → nothing up front; platform serves ads to people most likely to act; ideal for **conversions and revenue**; compare performance across platforms
	- **CPM (cost per thousand impressions)**: the amount you pay for every thousand impressions → ideal for **brand awareness**, delivering specific messages, engagement (when visibility matters more than click-through and conversion rates) (→ [Course 6: CPM of online vs. traditional ads](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
- Most platforms give a **recommended bid** based on your goal; analyze ad performance to adjust future bidding
#### Social media ad bidding
- Before posting an ad: set a **budget**, **target market**, and **bid strategy**
- **Ad auction**: the bidding process advertisers use to purchase ads (behind the scenes) → bid strategy = how much you're willing to pay for desired outcomes
	- Research **advertising benchmarks and average costs** for your business category and platforms → competitive starting range
	- The highest bid doesn't always win: algorithms consider the ad's **relevance, quality, and potential for user engagement**; bids are matched to **audience signals** that predict interaction rate
- Ad auction terms:
	- **Spend-based automated bidding**: set a daily budget to maximize your advertising goal
	- **Goal-based automated bidding**: set a **return on ad spend (ROAS)** or cost per action target to maximize your goal at a certain efficiency
	- **Manual bidding**: manage bids based on criteria you select
	- **Demographic targeting** (user information like age), **location targeting** (user location), **interest targeting** (user preferences)
	- **Maximum bid**: the highest amount you're willing to pay (the **ceiling**); **minimum bid**: the lowest amount allowed on a platform (the **floor**)
	- **Suggested bidding**: a recommended bid range for manual bidding
	- **Bid modification**: bid a percentage more or less than your starting bid
- Common bidding strategies:
	- **Cost per click (CPC)**: charged when someone clicks; usually with a **maximum CPC**
	- **Cost per action (CPA)**: charged when someone performs an action because of the ad (request a quote, join as a member, provide contact information, purchase) → requires **conversion tracking** on the website
		- Cost per action ≠ **cost per acquisition** (based on conversions only, i.e. actions defined as conversions) ≠ **customer acquisition cost (CAC)** (overall cost of acquiring a paying customer)
	- **Cost per mille (CPM)**: cost per one thousand impressions (views)
	- **Cost per view (CPV)**: charged only if a viewer watches a video ad for a minimum time or interacts with it (e.g. clicks an embedded link)
- By platform:
	- **Facebook**: automated, minimum ROAS, cost cap, bid cap
	- **X**: automatic, maximum bid, target bid
	- **LinkedIn**: maximum delivery, target cost, manual
	- **YouTube**: maximize conversions, target CPA, maximum CPV, target CPM
- Social media ad bidding: https://www.coursera.org/learn/from-likes-to-leads/supplement/ZuLo8

- **Manual bidding**: Managing bids based on the criteria the marketer selects

- **Pixel-based remarketing**: A process that sends ads automatically to users after placing a cookie into their web browser that tracks the pages and products they view
- **Remarketing**: A strategy in which a marketer uses paid ads to target customers who have visited a website, app, or social media profile
