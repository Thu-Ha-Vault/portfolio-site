#### What is email marketing?
- More than **four billion** people (over half the world's population) use email → why email marketing is so effective
- **Email marketing**: a company sends a branded email message to a list of their email subscribers in order to inform, drive sales, and create a community for their brand → promote services, products, goods, and stories to potential, new, and existing customers (→ [Course 7: Email for customer loyalty](/blog/satisfaction-guaranteed-develop-customer-loyalty-online/))
	- E.g. Miriam's favorite project: an email campaign to get shoppers excited about a plus-size fashion retailer's new seasonal styles, offering discounts
- **Return on investment (ROI)**: a ratio of net income (money made) and investment (money spent) → evaluates the efficiency of an investment; high ROI → made a lot more than you spent (→ [Course 5: ROI and ROAS calculations](/blog/assess-for-success-marketing-analytics-and-measurement/))
	- $42 earned per $1 spent → estimated ROI of **4,200%**
- Why email marketing is effective:
	- **Large number of users**: over four billion people
	- **Lifespan of email**: around for nearly half a century (first email in 1978); the Data and Marketing Association found **51%** of people use the same email for **over 10 years** → a subscriber could be your customer for 10 years
	- **Insights**: pieces of information discovered through research or data analysis that can be directly actioned upon to benefit your email strategy → just a few clicks away in email marketing automation platforms (Mailchimp, HubSpot, ...)
	- **Ease of use**: platforms like HubSpot, Mailchimp, Salesforce automate design, formatting, hyperlinking

### Preparing for your campaign
#### Conduct PESTLE and SWOT audits
- Spend significant time on the **strategy** part (a chef doesn't toss random ingredients into a pan; a marketer doesn't aimlessly send emails)
- Analyses before sending even one email → create **context** for your campaigns: be aware of your surroundings, internally and externally
	- **Internal** context (team, resources, who you are as a brand, play to your strengths, stay true to your mission) → **SWOT**
	- **External** context (so the brand isn't insensitive or unaware) → **PESTLE**
	- Conduct them on paper, in a document, on a whiteboard or shared document with the team → more voices = more diverse results
- **SWOT audit**: **strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats** → internal context; also useful for overall marketing, social, SEO strategy
	- **Strengths**: what makes the company's marketing resources stand out? What is special about the marketing team (eye-catching design, innovation, copywriting)?
	- **Weaknesses**: where could the company/team improve? (e.g. so talented with design and copywriting that strategy wasn't prioritized)
	- **Opportunities**: resources, events, or contacts that would help create an incredible campaign (e.g. a colleague's contact in another country → international brand awareness)
	- **Threats**: resources, events, contacts, opportunities that could be detrimental (e.g. a new competitor creating the exact same product)
	- Reflect on the results: general analysis, what you learned, where you shine, how to leverage it; compare with team members
- **PESTLE audit**: **political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental** factors that may affect your email marketing strategy → external context
	- **Political**: political unrest in a country you just started shipping to, new legislation that could impact your business
	- **Economic**: people in different locations prioritize spending on certain products → feature relevant products
	- **Social**: popular cultural references, social media use, population growth rates, geographic location, cultural aspects, age, education levels → an **inclusive** strategy
	- **Technological**: e.g. customers can ask smart devices to read emails aloud
	- **Legal**: laws in the markets you serve → certain tactics may not be lawful in some regions
	- **Environmental**: climate change, sustainability, natural disasters
- Next step: setting SMART goals
#### Learn more about conducting PESTLE and SWOT audits
- Same content as the video, as a guide; conduct audits before crafting the campaign and **repeat them throughout** the campaign; use clear header text to separate each section
- **SWOT** guiding questions:
	- **Strengths**: what makes marketing resources stand out, what is special about the team (eye-catching design, innovative ideas, strong copywriting), what the company excels at internally
	- **Weaknesses**: where to improve, which aspects of the brand have been neglected, what to prioritize more, what training is required, where you're underperforming
	- **Opportunities**: resources, events, or contacts that would help; opportunities to leverage to improve process or results
	- **Threats**: harmful events, contacts, opportunities; competitors; whether you have the resources to complete tasks
	- Reflect: general thoughts or trends, what you learned, how to use it
- **PESTLE** guiding questions:
	- **Political**: recent changes in governing bodies, political unrest, new legislation
	- **Economic**: state of local and international economies, what people prioritize spending on
	- **Social**: popular culture events, education levels, traditions and cultural norms, how people interact in the regions you serve
	- **Technological**: how technology changes the way people consume and interact; changes in the last 6 months / 1 year
	- **Legal**: regulations and laws in the regions served, how they differ across regions
	- **Environmental**: what it means to be a sustainable company, importance of climate change to customers, recent natural disasters
- More research and brainstorming before the campaign → more information to create a successful campaign

#### Set SMART goals
- **SMART goals**: **specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, time-bound** → for the overall campaign or more specifically for social, SEO, or email marketing (→ [Course 1: How to set SMART goals](/blog/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/))
- **Specific**: when you've made it to your destination, you know you've arrived
	- Questions: what do I want to accomplish? Why is this a goal (reason, purpose, benefit)? Who is involved, who is the recipient? Where should it be delivered? Requirements and constraints?
	- Example (app-based tech company): "grow the brand" is not specific → add a certain number of names to the subscriber list over the course of the campaign
- **Measurable**: what evidence will prove whether it was successful? → how much, how many, how will I know when it's accomplished?
	- E.g. increase app downloads: 100? 1,000? 10,000? → choose a number you can track
	- **Metrics**: what you use to measure something (e.g. miles for a 2,789.4-mile drive from Los Angeles to New York City); email marketing example: **ROI** (average 4,200%)
- **Attainable**: realistic but somewhat challenging
	- E.g. company gets about **300 downloads a week** → tripling in the first month is unattainable → weekly growth of **1.5 times** (450 downloads per week) one month into the campaign
- **Relevant**: does it line up with company values and company-wide goals, the organization's needs and priorities?
- **Time-bound**: a deadline → know if you're making progress at a good pace; time and metrics go hand-in-hand (time can be a metric)
	- Campaign CTA: download the app and share the email with friends; runs **three months**
	- Final goal: *increase weekly app downloads by 450 downloads over a three-month span*
#### Create an email marketing strategy
- **Email marketing strategy**: a set of procedures a marketer identifies and follows to achieve desired marketing goals with email advertising (record it on paper or in Word/Google Docs)
- Sections:
	1. **Set your goals**: often in line with business goals → increase brand visibility, website traffic, sales; acquire new customers; build relationships with existing customers; increase brand loyalty
	2. **Choose an email marketing provider**: **email marketing provider** (email marketing service) = a company that offers email marketing or bulk email services → Mailchimp, HubSpot, Salesforce, Constant Contact
		- Factors: **cost, deliverability, reporting, email list management, template features**
	3. **Build your email marketing subscriber list**: an ongoing process → signup form on your website, in-person signup list, signup form on social media
		- **Segmentation**: divide the list into smaller groups based on interests, location, purchase history → target specific groups for specific goals (→ [Course 1: email segmentation](/blog/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/))
	4. **Evaluate brand guidelines**: **brand guidelines** = rules and standards that convey how a brand should be represented, apply to all content including emails → fonts, colors, layouts, illustrations, logos; templates apply them to all outgoing emails
	5. **Determine email frequency**: how often emails reach subscribers (schedule covered later)
	6. **Determine performance measuring methods**: basic reporting tracks emails opened, unsubscribers, **click-through rate (CTR)**; some monitor **bounce rate** (percentage of emails that have not successfully reached recipients); **deliverability check** (are emails getting to intended recipients?) → state which tools and when you'll collect and evaluate them

### Do's and don’ts of email marketing
#### The do's and don'ts of email marketing
- Many decisions depend on who you target and what works best for them, but some tried and true do's and don'ts:
- Over **300 billion** emails are sent every day (expert estimate)
- **Do prioritize quality over quantity**: set a standard and define what quality means for your brand or team → if people like the content, they look forward to emails even once a week
	- Quality may mean enriching their life, interesting or beautiful images, teaching something (e.g. a mental health company sends a new resource or exercise each Monday morning)
- **Don't spam**: **spam** = unsolicited and unwanted junk email sent out in bulk to a broad recipient list
	- Don't appear in inboxes several times a week just to stay top-of-mind; spam uses dramatic phrases and too-good-to-be-true offers → ends up in the spam folder
- **Do segment your lists**: **segmenting** = dividing an email subscriber list into smaller groups based on criteria like interests, location, or purchase history → tailored emails
	- E.g. segment by gender (women, men, non-binary, other self-identified genders) for a product that sells better to a certain gender
	- E.g. a **10% birthday discount** → segment by birthday month
	- **Segmenting by time zone** (popular) → land in inboxes first thing in the morning
- **Don't send the same emails to every subscriber** → they won't identify with them, less likely to read, may unsubscribe
- **Do test** various formats, lengths, links, and images → almost impossible to get it right the first time (e.g. hardly anyone clicked the hyperlinks → link to different content or give more context)
- **Don't stick to the same format** without testing anything new
#### Aaron - Do's and don’ts for email marketing
- Aaron: growth marketing manager at Google → owned and operated marketing services (news, information, resources about Google tools)
- Started with specific campaigns: defining creative and copy, what to experiment with and test to improve success metrics, getting campaigns out the door
- Best practices:
	- Content **personalized and customized** → usable almost immediately in their day to day
	- **Less is more**: people are crushed under campaigns from every company → get your message and desired action to the user fast
	- **Constantly be testing**: best practices from online or friends may not resonate with your audience → **A/B testing** with different variants, look closely at defined success metrics, build on what works
	- Don't let emails get **stale and static** → keep experimenting, integrate new technologies and innovations (stay on the bleeding edge)
- Advice for entry-level roles: subscribe to and study emails from companies you follow → how they differ in style, content, length; which ones you enjoy and interact with most
#### Ethical email language
- **Ethical email marketing**: creating strategies that bring true value to your audience while maintaining the moral principles your business subscribes to → one consideration: choose your language carefully
- Unethical language → caught by **spam filters** (never reaches mailboxes) or creates negative feelings and distrust; manipulative, exaggerated, unethical, or desperate words put brand voice and integrity at risk
- **Use trustworthy and honest language**: genuine, conversational, in the brand voice; exclude time-sensitive words ("10% off inside" beats "URGENT: Act Now")
	- Flagged by spam filters: *Act now, Apply now, Urgent, Exclusive deal, Important information regarding, This won't last*
- **Be truthful about what you're offering**: state exactly what the offer is (e.g. an extra 50% off sale items); avoid hyperbolic phrases
	- *Best offer ever, Fantastic deal, Free money, No catch, No fees*
- **Use language that adds value**: avoid desperate language; use "we think you'll like this", "we made this with you in mind"
	- Avoid: *Please read, We need your help, You need to see this*
- Not sure? Test things out and analyze what your audience responds well to

## Module 2: Types of email marketing
### Emails that welcome new subscribers
#### Welcome to module 2
- Module 2 roadmap: skills to craft successful emails, examples of eye-catching copy; content changes based on what you hope to achieve → common email types (→ [Course 1: Types of email campaigns](/blog/foundations-of-digital-marketing-and-e-commerce/)):
	- **Acquisition emails**: acquiring new customers
	- **Welcome emails**: welcome new clients and introduce yourself as a brand
	- **Newsletters**: tell stories, fill clients in on relevant offers or anecdotes from within the company
	- **Promotional emails**: let customers know about current sales or offers
	- **Retention emails**: keep customers coming back
#### Types of email marketing: Acquisition emails
- **Acquisition emails**: emails sent to acquire (bring in) new customers → integral to company growth; turn a potential customer into an existing customer
	- Marketing funnel: **awareness** stage (create buzz, market research, grow the brand by acquiring new customers)
- Secret: give the potential customer something **before they even know they want it**
	- Material: downloadable guides, product catalogs, PDFs, free trials
	- Or: an indirect compliment, positivity, new or educational information, a glimpse into what the product will give them
- **Email copy**: the text in your email and subject line → witty, unique, and inclusive copy is a huge difference maker (often sent to brand new recipients who have never come in contact with your brand)
- Examples:
	- Social media photo and video editing company: tutorial on attention-grabbing social media videos (seasonality, mood) → offers something new, short and straight to the offer, light and vibrant copy, wordplay "Spring into action with video"
	- Cosmetics company: **interactive** email to match products to skincare needs → personalized information + offer to act; clear **call-to-action** throughout (banner with **10% off**, small "Shop Now" button under each item); subject line "Our number 1 most asked question" acts as a **teaser**
#### Types of email marketing: Welcome emails
- **Welcome emails**: emails sent to brand new customers or subscribers → a virtual **first impression** (be thoughtful, like welcoming someone into your home)
	- Marketing funnel: **consideration** stage (users have elected to subscribe, looking for more information) → nurture your leads
- Ask: what do you want people to know about you? Which aspects of the business's identity must be included?
- A good welcome email includes:
	- A **conversational and inviting tone**
	- A **brief summary** of what makes your brand unique or your brand's story
	- A **call-to-action (CTA)**: a concise instruction that tells the customer what to do next (from "buy a product" to a subtle "Get started" button to a landing page to create an account) → subtle or direct depending on what subscribers engage with
- When sent: thank first-time subscribers or customers who purchased, after downloading an app, to encourage a purchase with a discount code
- Example: kitchenware company → free shipping code at the top (more likely to click "Shop tableware"), a letter from the founder about the company's background (personal, conversational; goal to make all meals feel special), CTAs to follow on social media and shop online
### Emails that entice subscribers
#### Types of email marketing: Newsletters
- **Newsletter**: an email sent to subscribers on a regular basis, containing news and informational content relevant to the company and of interest to subscribers
	- Advertise your product, build trust and show expertise, create lasting relationships, inform and educate
	- Marketing funnel: **consideration**, **conversion**, and **loyalty** stages → incredibly versatile
- What to include:
	- **Only relevant content** readers will enjoy: big company news, new product or service launches, updates; external resources too (e.g. a wellness company shares articles on anxiety-reducing breathing exercises from reputable therapists); some of the best newsletters include articles relevant to subscribers' lifestyles without selling
	- **Trending topics**, pop culture, current events → informed and fresh
	- **Catchy, clever writing** → stand out among dozens of emails; style and tone on brand and genuine (e.g. hospital → empathetic; rideshare company → casual, humorous)
	- Clean, **on-brand design**
- Example: a furniture company's **bi-monthly** newsletter → headline "Pull up a chair" (inviting tone), refers to a current event that kept people at home, a chair for each type of subscriber (remote worker → office chair, new family addition → rocking chair for a nursery, poolside lounger → outdoor lounge chair) linked to landing pages; copy on comfort levels, dimensions, product details
#### Craft catchy newsletter copy
- **Write to add value**: every email should add value (new products/services, making them laugh, teaching something) → each element thoughtful and intentional (→ [Course 7: Email copy with AI](/blog/satisfaction-guaranteed-develop-customer-loyalty-online/))
- **Subject line**: the first text recipients see after your name in the inbox → first impression
	- **Keep it brief**: about **6–10 words** (otherwise cut off)
	- **Pique curiosity**: e.g. for abandoned carts, "These items are too good to leave in your cart."
	- **Be clear** if you're offering something (experience, information, discount)
	- **Consider personalizing** with first names (depends on the email marketing tool)
- **Preview text**: next to the subject line in the inbox, gives extra insight into what's inside
	- Include the **most important piece of information**
	- **Align** with the subject line
	- Sometimes maintain a sense of **mystery** (e.g. "The recipe you didn't know you needed…") if it fits your goals and brand
	- Keep it between **35–50 characters**
- **Body**: where most content is; test different approaches
	- **Second person perspective** ("you" language): "Here's a discount for you" > "Here's a discount for our readers"
	- Break up blocks of text with **white space**
	- A **compelling call to action** (newsletters may have several)
- Craft catchy newsletter copy: https://www.coursera.org/learn/think-outside-the-inbox/supplement/qcrzg
#### Types of email marketing: Promotional emails
- **Promotional emails**: emails sent to inform subscribers of new or existing products or services
	- Reasons: sitewide sale, **10% off** for customers' birthdays, celebrating five years in business, launching a new product, contest or giveaway, free downloadable content for a holiday
	- Goals: speed up the buying process, encourage action, create new or repeat customers → **consideration** and **loyalty** stages
- Tips (no strict rules):
	- **Keep the focus on the promotion** itself, not product features (link to a landing page for details)
	- **Keep it concise**: **body** = text in the main content of the email → readable in a couple of minutes
	- **Announce the promotion in the subject line**: **47%** of emails are opened based on the subject line alone → hint something special is inside, relevant to the promotion
- Example: grocery delivery app → banner with a free delivery code on scones from the bakery, "Order Now" button, subject line "Free delivery on something sweet"
### Emails that keep customers
#### Types of email marketing: Retention emails
- **Retention email**: an email sent to a current customer with the intent of keeping them as a customer → all about **timing** (→ [Course 6: Tailored offers](/blog/make-the-sale-build-launch-and-manage-e-commerce-stores/))
	- Marketing funnel: **loyalty** stage (active customers; keep customers happy)
- Types:
	- **Cart abandonment email**: customer puts an item in the online cart but doesn't purchase → follow-up encouraging purchase
	- **Feedback** request after a purchase → customers feel heard, improvements made
	- **Special offer** for users who aren't engaging with newsletters or promotional emails
- Retention emails should include:
	- **Personalization**: get to know your customers and tailor emails based on who they are; **segmentation** = categorizing contacts by a predefined set of identifiers so each email feels relevant → first name in emails and subject lines, follow-up tips on using the purchased product
	- **A clear call to action**: buy again (discount code), sign up for a new loyalty program
	- **Empathetic and inviting language**: "How did we do and how is your experience?" (maybe a separate feedback email, or a 1–10 scale at the bottom); **thank you language** → customers feel appreciated (→ [Course 7: Post-purchase follow-up](/blog/satisfaction-guaranteed-develop-customer-loyalty-online/))
- Example: vacation rental and lodging site → individualized highlights for every host, "Get your host highlights" button, grateful language ("we can't thank you enough")
#### Create accessible emails
- Create content every audience can experience equally, including people with **sensory disabilities** (affect one or more of a person's senses: hearing, vision, or both)
- **Accessibility**: the design of products, devices, services, or environments for people with disabilities
	- E.g. captions or a transcript for videos → people with auditory disabilities, and anyone in a loud place or a library
	- Inaccessible emails reflect negatively on a brand → lower **open rate** and **click rate** over time, missed revenue
- **Assistive technology**: helps people with disabilities perform tasks (wheelchairs, hearing aids)
	- For email: **screen enlargement application** (magnifies text and images) and **screen reader** (converts text, buttons, images, and other screen elements into speech or Braille) → reads invisible code like headers, images, links
	- **Alt text** (alternative text): a brief, written description of an image, primarily to assist individuals who are visually impaired → not shown visually, read aloud by a screen reader
- Best practices:
	- **Formatting**: organize headings thoughtfully (header tags); avoid all capital letters and excessive italic/underline
	- **Fonts**: size at least **14**; bold or larger font for headings; simple **sans serif** fonts (no decorative strokes, wider spacing → easier for low vision, dyslexia); careful with **emojis** (avoid in subject lines and for important information)
	- **Colors**: contrasting text and background (**black text on white** is most effective); don't rely solely on color (color blindness)
	- **Images**: text in images only if necessary; alt text for images critical to the message (informative, actionable); **omit alt text for decorative images** (logos, lifestyle images, icons)
	- **Links**: clear purpose → "Learn more about our sale" instead of "Click here," "Go," "Get started"
- Test with the computer's built-in screen reader (accessibility settings) or an online accessibility checker; resource: Google Accessibility

#### Spamming: When not to send emails
- Sending too frequently reflects poorly on your business; in **2021**, nearly **320 billion** emails were sent worldwide daily → subscribers overwhelmed may unsubscribe
- Questions for setting **email frequency**:
	- **How large is your subscriber list?**
		- Fewer than **500** → once a month
		- **500–10,000** → once a week
		- **10,000 or more** → twice a week
	- **What purpose is this email serving?** (educating on a new product may need only one email)
	- **What types of emails?** (newsletters more frequent than promotional emails)
	- **What content?** (nearly identical content → no need to send more than once; a sale → one when it starts, one when it's about to end)
- **Ask your audience**: survey at signup on how often they want emails, a welcome email asking for feedback, an unsubscribe survey offering less frequent emails (once a month, once a week) → fewer unsubscribes

### How to write an effective email
#### Write effective emails
- The most important part is the **content** → beautiful design doesn't matter if the copy isn't compelling, informative, or adding value
- **Subject line**: answers "what are you offering?" → prioritize **clarity over catchiness**, then add a little excitement (→ [Course 7: Wayfair subject lines](/blog/satisfaction-guaranteed-develop-customer-loyalty-online/))
	- E.g. an emerging online audiobook and podcast company: "Books for your ears" (mission, concise) or "Bookworms you've been waiting for us" (target audience)
- **Body**: how can this content help your reader? What stories can you tell?
	- **Second person**: point of view for giving directions, offering advice, explanations → you, your, yours (except when using their name)
	- **Personalize**: **merge tag** / **personalization tag** = a code that inserts unique user data from the mailing list into emails (e.g. `{{ fname }}` → "Hey, Angelique, what's your favorite book? We've got it.")
	- **Benefits rather than features**: "if you like to listen to your books... Listen on your way to work, at the gym or while you're doing the dishes"
	- **Be brief**: short and succinct → readers stay interested
- **Call to action** after the body: e.g. a clickable button "Try it for free now"
#### More tips on writing effective emails
- **Purpose**: why are you sending this email? (product launch, tips and tricks, discount code, sale) → creates context, easier to decide what to say
- **Narrative**: what kind of narrative do subscribers want to read? (think from the readers' perspective)
	- Product launch → the story of how the idea came to life (who, motivation, how long, what problem it solves)
	- Weekly educational newsletter → a **theme** each week, internal and external resources that fit → cohesive
	- Sale → why there's a sale and how it benefits them (company birthday, holiday, clearing inventory, end-of-season)
- **Tone**: always courteous and helpful, plus qualities based on the goal
	- Product launch → bright and enthusiastic
	- Weekly newsletter → professional and light, authoritative (you're the expert)
	- Sale or brand birthday → spunky and thankful (gratitude)
	- Pro tip: **read your emails aloud**; the tone must fit your **brand voice**
- Determine purpose, narrative, and tone before writing and refer back to them

### Mistakes in email marketing
#### Mistakes in email marketing
- Emails can't be edited once sent → take **preventative measures** to identify and fix mistakes before sending
- Common mistakes and fixes:
	- **Broken link**: a hyperlink that no longer leads to the correct website (reader gets an error) → double check every hyperlink, have a colleague review; fix: **redirect** the typo URL to the correct one, or let it go and triple check next time
	- **Wrong segmented list** (e.g. an email for 18–25 year olds sent to the 40–55 group) → a **quality control (QC)** process = a process through which a business seeks to ensure that product quality is maintained or improved → e.g. a checklist for format, content, personalization tags
	- **Wrong or outdated email**: be an expert user of your automation tools; keep **apology templates** ready → admit the mistake, apologize, offer something
		- E.g. "We messed up, we're sorry. We sent an email claiming a product was on sale, but that product is in fact out of stock and the sale ended last week. To make it up to you, we're offering you 10% off the entire store for today only."
	- **Typos** (most common), especially in **personalization tags** ("Here's a gift for you, Malcolm" vs. a misspelled tag showing the raw code) → automated spellcheck (online or a browser plugin like Grammarly), proofread several times, send a **test email** to yourself
	- **Spam folders**: not adhering to your country's spam-related laws, or the email seems like spam (too many images, images too large) → send a test email first
#### How to fix email marketing mistakes
- Everyone makes mistakes; how you react matters → a checklist (**contingency plan**):
	- **Leverage the experts** at your company: have team members made the same mistake? What did they do?
	- **Consider sending a corrected version**: a minor typo may not warrant it; a pressing error (incorrect product launch date or sale date) might → make the change clear (bold or color)
	- **Offer an incentive** if the mistake was really bad: free shipping, extending a sale, a discount code
	- **Broken link** → redirect to the correct one as soon as possible (helps those who haven't clicked yet)
	- **Apologize on social media or in a public statement** if someone may have been upset or offended → depends on business size, severity, existing company protocol
- Have a thorough contingency plan in place ahead of time

### Most common email marketing metrics
#### Common email marketing metrics
- **Metrics**: quantifiable measurements used to track and assess progress towards a business objective → know each one whether you set KPIs or they're set for you
- **Open rate**: the percentage of users that open your email = people who opened / people who received (usually automated by tools)
	- Indicates how engaged subscribers are; high → effective subject lines, low → subject lines need work
	- Some companies care more about engagement after opening than opens
- **Click-to-open rate**: the percentage of email recipients who clicked on one or more links in an email out of the total number of unique opens = total clicks / unique opens (→ [Course 5: Plan A/B tests on click-to-open rate](/blog/assess-for-success-marketing-analytics-and-measurement/))
	- Often used in **A/B testing** (two versions sent at the same time to see which the audience responds to more positively) (→ [Course 3: A/B testing](/blog/from-likes-to-leads-interact-with-customers-online/))
	- E.g. a new cleaning business sends two versions with different images attached to hyperlinks → the version with the higher click-to-open rate guides future image choices
- **Unsubscribe rate**: the percentage of recipients who unsubscribe from your send list after opening an email = unsubscribes / emails delivered → are you delivering content your audience enjoys?
	- Some users just stop opening instead of unsubscribing → a different issue
- Whether these are the right KPIs is up to you and your team
#### Common email marketing metrics: Part 2
- **Conversion rate**: the percentage of recipients who clicked on a link in your email and took a desired action, like making a purchase → "did your audience do the thing you wanted them to do?"
	- High conversion rate → selling more without increasing budget
	- = conversions / clicks; usually from analytics tools like **Google Analytics** (marketing statistics and analytical insights) (→ [Course 5: Email conversion rate in Google Analytics](/blog/assess-for-success-marketing-analytics-and-measurement/))
	- E.g. house cleaning company → how many people booked services from the emails
- **Email bounce rate**: the percentage of emails sent that could not be delivered to the recipient's inbox = (bounced emails / emails sent) × 100
	- Varies → compare with **industry standards** online; higher than average → investigate
	- Causes: incorrect or inactive email addresses, an import error with your email list
	- Avoid: verify your domain, keep the automation tool up to date and working, **purge your list periodically**, have subscribers' permission
- **Complaint rate**: the percentage of complaints recipients send to mailbox providers about receiving your email = complaints / emails delivered
	- Industry standard about **0.01%** (one complaint per 1,000 emails); a small business may not track it for a while
	- Gauges how happy subscribers are (not offending them or getting something wrong)
#### Common email marketing metrics: Part 3
- Growth is defined differently by each organization → one tracks list growth rate, another forward rate, another ROI
- **ROI**: ratio of money made and money spent = (total revenue / total spent) × 100
	- E.g. house cleaning company made **$800** from its first campaign, spent about **$200** → ROI **400%**
	- Email marketing ROI tends to be high because total spend is low; especially useful in reports and presentations (a dollar amount shows effectiveness)
	- Reviewed less frequently: other metrics daily or weekly, ROI **monthly, quarterly, or annually**
	- Average **42:1** ($42 per $1), but returns depend on sales volume, average order value, website conversion rate, industry
- **List growth rate**: the rate at which your list grows = [(new subscribers − unsubscribers) / total email addresses] × 100 → with quality emails, higher list growth → higher sales volume → higher ROI
- **Forward rate**: the percentage of recipients who click the share button to post to social media or the forward button = forwards or shares / emails delivered → generates **new leads**; a compliment to your brand
#### Learn more about common email marketing metrics
- Metrics to measure depend on business goals and campaign goals; tools track them, but you can calculate them yourself:

| Metric | Definition | Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open rate** | % of users who open your email (engagement; subject line effectiveness) | Number of people who open the email / Number of emails delivered |
| **Click-to-open rate** | % of recipients who clicked one or more links (used in A/B testing) | Total clicks / Number of unique opens |
| **Unsubscribe rate** | % of recipients who unsubscribe after opening an email | Number of unsubscribes / Number of emails delivered |
| **Complaint rate** | % of complaints recipients send to mailbox providers | Number of complaints / Total number of emails delivered to subscribers |
| **Conversion rate** | % of recipients who clicked a link and took a desired action | Number of conversions / Number of emails delivered |
| **Forward rate** | % of recipients who share to social media or forward | Number of forwards / Number of emails delivered |
| **List growth rate** | The rate at which your list grows | [(Number of new subscribers − number of unsubscribes) / Total number of email addresses] × 100 |
| **Bounce rate** | % of emails sent that could not be delivered | (Total number of bounced emails / Emails sent) × 100 |
| **Campaign ROI** | Ratio of money made and money spent | (Total revenue / Total spent) × 100 |

- Note: the reading divides conversion rate by emails delivered, while the video divides by clicks
- Pulling insights from your metrics is how you achieve your marketing goals

### Email marketing results
#### Create an email marketing report
- Share tracked metrics and campaign success with stakeholders and marketing team members → how effective campaigns are, whether time is used wisely
- **Email marketing report**: a collection of your KPIs presented to your team and stakeholders to inform them of your campaign's progress
	- Answers: how has the business benefited from email marketing? Is the strategy falling flat anywhere? How can we improve? Can we put more money behind certain aspects?
- Presenting reports: be **concise, visual, engaging, and show growth** (→ [Course 3: Present a social media report](/blog/from-likes-to-leads-interact-with-customers-online/))
	- **Concise**: value stakeholders' time; only necessary details; avoid jargon and technical language
	- **Visual**: e.g. a line graph with spikes and dips beats text explaining them; reports straight from automation software are OK, or tailor with **Google Slides, Microsoft PowerPoint, Canva**; images, diagrams, charts
	- **Engaging**: vary how you present data → videos of demos, testimonials, case studies; a story or anecdote; audience participation (questions, surveys, quizzes)
	- **Show growth**: include key performance areas → describe goals and objectives (e.g. grow the subscriber list for a house cleaning service) → how you met them against KPIs (e.g. goal: increase **list growth rate by 400% over four months** → did you hit it?) as a bullet point list; also lessons learned and areas for improvement
#### Karan - Tips for presenting to stakeholders
- Karan: Digital Strategy Lead on Google's **Accelerated Growth** team → internal startup incubator/accelerator for high-potential customers (new funding, busy season, new locations, new sales reps) → betas, resources, consultants
- Presenting can be scary → start with a real conversation (How's life? How's your day?) → stakeholders are real people
- **Feel prepared**: know what you'll talk about, anticipate objections, work toward clear action items and next steps
	- Prepare an **agenda**; know **who's in the room** → customize the content
- **Prepare stakeholders beforehand**: meet each one individually before the group presentation → get opinions, "vibe check," make sure they're aligned and will support you
- **Follow up**: a detailed email with everything discussed → action items, owners, timelines, next follow-up; put everything in writing
- **Poor or unexpected results**: tackle the issue head on, acknowledge it before they do, bring tangible action items and solutions → proactive, not reactive ("this is why it's happening, and these are the three next steps")
- Advice for a first presentation: **don't present too much, less is more** → decide the key takeaways and work backwards
#### Tips on presenting
- Public speaking makes many people nervous → more practice, more natural
- **Tip 1: Speak clearly and slowly** → pace yourself (e.g. tap your toe inside your shoe to keep a rhythm)
- **Tip 2: Allow for pauses** → show confidence and set the pace (after posing a question, when transitioning sections)
- **Tip 3: Make eye contact** → one person for **3–5 seconds**, then the next; alternatives: look at foreheads or just past people at the wall; virtually → look at the webcam, post notes beside the screen
- **Tip 4: Tell a story** → beginning, middle, end; each slide pushes the story forward; include real users (quotes, photos, short video clips) → emotions, pain points, delights
- **Tip 5: Be concise** → average human attention span is **eight seconds** (a study)
- **Tip 6: Know your audience** → tone and terminology fit the audience (colleagues vs. external clients); be yourself → authenticity and trust (→ [Course 5: Best practices for presenting data](/blog/assess-for-success-marketing-analytics-and-measurement/))
