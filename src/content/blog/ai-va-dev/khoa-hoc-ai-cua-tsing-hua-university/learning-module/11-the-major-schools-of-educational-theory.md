## The Conception of Educational Practice
1. The nature of educational practice: its distinctions from, and connections with, other forms of social practice, such as productive practice, political practice, scientific research, artistic practice, and medical practice
![Ảnh chụp Màn hình 2026-09-13 lúc 20.31.22](/vault-assets/anh-chup-man-hinh-2026-09-13-luc-20-31-22-e9bc056f.png)
2. The Elements of educational practice: Subject, Object, Purpose, Method, Content, and the Question of What Counts as "Education" or "Good Education"
	- **Subject of Practice (Who)**: Who provides education? Who is qualified to serve as an educator? What conditions should individuals meet to be selected as educators? How can the desired qualities and abilities be cultivated in future educators? How should educators be empowered and regulated?... These are not trivial questions. The quality of education depends enormously on the quality of those who deliver it.
	- **Object of Practice (Whom)**: Who receives education? Should education be universal, covering all children, or selective, targeting only some? If selective, which groups should be prioritized, and on what grounds? What characteristics define those who are identified as needing education, and how should they be treated? These questions have profound ethical and social implications.
	- **Purpose of Practice (Why)**: Why is education necessary at all? What transformations should education bring about in learners? When multiple educational goals exist and potentially conflict with one another, how should priorities be determined? This element forces us to think carefully about the values that underlie educational decisions.
	- **Content of Practice (What)**: What should be taught? Through what knowledge can educational goals be achieved? How is this knowledge selected, and what criteria justify those selections? These are questions that curriculum designers and educators grapple with constantly, and they are never purely technical — they always involve value judgments about what knowledge matters most.
	- **Method or Approaches of Practice (How)**: What types of education should be provided? How should teaching and learning be carried out? How should educators handle the various tensions and contradictions that arise in the learning process? And how does the application of technology reshape educational practice? These methodological questions are at the heart of pedagogical research.
	- **What is "Education" or "Good Education"**:  How should educational outcomes be evaluated? What kind of ideal education is worth pursuing, and why? This question runs through all the other elements and reminds us that educational practice is never value-neutral — it always embodies a vision of what human flourishing looks like.
3. Types of educational practice (Two dimensions for classifying types of practice: individual-social and institutionalized-non-institutionalized; four types)
	- Historical Evolution of Forms of Educational Practice: over time, educational practice has generally evolved from predominantly non-institutionalized and individual forms toward increasingly institutionalized and social forms.

|                       | Individual | Social   |
| --------------------- | ---------- | -------- |
| Non-institutionalized | Type I (V) | Type II  |
| Institutionalized     | Type IV    | Type III |
		- Type I refers to non-institutionalized, individual educational practice — think of a parent tutoring a child at home, or a mentor guiding an apprentice in an informal setting. 
		- Type II is non-institutionalized but social in character — for example, community-based learning or informal cultural transmission within a social group. 
		- Type III is institutionalized and social — this includes large-scale public education systems, national curricula, and formal schooling at the societal level. 
		- Type IV, highlighted here, is institutionalized and individual — such as formal schooling directed at individual students within established educational institutions. This is the form of educational practice most familiar to us today.
4. The Logic of educational practice (General form or structure): "The logic of education practice refers to the *general form, structure, or generative principles of educational practical actions. It represents the internal laws* that make the various forms of educational practice possible and that are commonly shared or followed by them. Habitus give educational practice its historical character, while contexts continuously reshape the intentions of practice. *The structures of time and space* are also intrinsically related to practical actions. *Educational practice is not a process of pure rational activity; rather, it has its own unique logic of the field*" (Shi, 2006)
	- habitus — a concept that refers to the deeply ingrained dispositions and habits that practitioners develop over time — gives educational practice its historical character. We do not come to educational practice as blank slates; we bring with us accumulated traditions, norms, and ways of doing things.
	- the contexts in which practice occurs continuously reshape the intentions and meanings of practical actions. The same action can mean very different things in different contexts. Third, the structures of time and space are intrinsically related to practical actions — when and where education happens matters enormously.
	- educational practice is not a process of pure rational activity. It is not simply a matter of applying a set of logical rules to achieve predetermined outcomes. Rather, it has its own unique logic of the field — a logic that is shaped by history, context, human relationships, and the irreducible complexity of human development.
## Understanding Educational Policy
1. Educational Policy: 
	- **Definition:** Educational policy refers to a normative system formulated by educational leaders based on certain principles, concepts, and laws, with the purpose of guiding, regulating, constraining, and transforming educational practices within a specific scope. It includes educational laws, regulations, institutional systems, and other formal documents issued through administrative authorization.
	- **Functions:** Educational policy is one of the important factors influencing and constraining all educational practices (macro, meso, and micro levels). It affects the elements, structures, processes, and outcomes of educational activities.
	- **Implications:** In today’s era of highly institutionalized educational practice, engaging in, understanding, and transforming educational practice requires first understanding and transforming educational policies.

2. Type of Educational Policy
	- (1) According to China’s administrative divisions, educational policies can be classified into the following levels: national educational policies — provincial (municipality directly under the central government/autonomous region) educational policies — regional educational policies — county-level educational policies — township educational policies.
	- (2) According to the educational work content that educational policies aim to guide, educational policies can be categorized into educational financial policies, educational personnel policies, enrollment policies, textbook policies, educational language policies, etc.
	- (3) According to the types of education to which educational policies apply, educational policies can be divided into basic education policies, preschool education policies, secondary education policies, higher education policies, special education policies, vocational education policies, social education policies, etc.
3. Formulation and Implementation
- **(1) Factors That Must Be Considered in Educational Policy Formulation**: Policy issues, policy concepts, policy objectives, policy environment, policy procedures, policy documents, policy conditions, policy risks, etc.
- **(2) General Process of Educational Policy Implementation**: Policy issuance → Policy dissemination → Policy implementation or execution → Policy evaluation → Policy revision, etc.
## Understanding Educational Theory
1. What is "Educational Theory"?
	- (1) The relationship between “educational theory,” “educational experience,” “educational slogans,” and “educational viewpoints”; individual educational theoretical propositions and educational theoretical systems: Experience refers to what practitioners learn from doing; slogans are catchy phrases that may or may not have theoretical grounding; viewpoints are individual opinions or perspectives.Theory, by contrast, involves systematic, evidence-based, logically coherent propositions about education. We should also distinguish between individual theoretical propositions — specific claims about how education works — and full educational theoretical systems, which organize multiple propositions into a coherent whole.
	- (2) The distinction between “Theory of Education” and “Theories about Education.”: A Theory of Education is a comprehensive, systematic account of education as a whole, while theories about education may be partial accounts drawn from other disciplines — psychology, sociology, philosophy — that shed light on specific aspects of educational phenomena. (→ [Sociology of Education](/blog/ai-va-dev/khoa-hoc-ai-cua-tsing-hua-university/learning-module/09-sociology-of-education/), [Educational Psychology](/blog/ai-va-dev/khoa-hoc-ai-cua-tsing-hua-university/learning-module/12-educational-psychology/))
	- (3) Foreign educational theories and indigenous educational theories.
2. Criteria for a "Better" Educational Theory
	- (1) Sufficient evidence: it cannot simply be speculation or wishful thinking.
	- (2) Strong logical consistency: its propositions must cohere with one another and not contradict each other.
	- (3) Strong explanatory power: it should be able to account for a wide range of educational phenomena, not just a narrow set of cases.
	- (4) Acceptability of its value positions or claims: since education is a normative enterprise, the values embedded in a theory matter and must be defensible.
	- (5) Good verifiability, predictability, and reproducibility: its claims should be testable and its predictions should hold up across different contexts.
	- (6) Good public nature: it should be transparent, accessible, and open to scrutiny by the broader scholarly community.
3. The dual nature of Educational Theory
	- (1) Autonomy and openness: It has its own disciplinary identity and internal logic, but it also draws on insights from many other fields.
	- (2) Scientific nature and value orientation: Unlike the natural sciences, educational theory cannot be purely value-free — it always embodies commitments about what education is for and what counts as good education.
	- (3) Theoretical nature and practical nature: Good educational theory should illuminate practice, and good practice should inform theory. The two are not separate worlds.
	- (4) Local characteristics and international perspective: It must be grounded in the specific cultural, historical, and social contexts in which education takes place, while also engaging with the broader global conversation about education.
## The practice and Policy Values of Educational Theory
- Educational theory helps transform or reconstruct the limited educational experiences of educational practitioners and policymakers;
- Educational theory helps enhance the rational awareness of educational practitioners and policymakers;
- Educational theory helps broaden the knowledge and value perspectives of educational practitioners and policymakers;
- Educational theory helps shape the educational character of educational practitioners, policymakers, and researchers.
## The Relationship Among Educational Theory, Educational Practice, and Educational Policy
- Educational theory is an intrinsic component of educational practice and educational policy actions. There is no practice or policy action that is completely free from theoretical guidance.
- For educational practice and educational policy, educational theory is not the only influencing factor, but it is one of the most important factors.
- Practice without theoretical support can only become blind action; policies without theoretical support can only become short-sighted policies.
- Educational theory is not merely an “embellishment” of educational practice and policy; rather, it is the “vitamin” that sustains educational theory and policy. One of the most important characteristics of modern education is the establishment and continuous enhancement of theoretical awareness.

## What Is an Independent Knowledge System of the Educational Discipline?
- Educational discipline is an open and broad-spectrum field of knowledge. In terms of its nature, it possesses characteristics including autonomy and openness, locality and internationality, theoretical nature and practicality, as well as inheritance and innovation.
- An “independent knowledge system of the educational discipline” refers, on the one hand, to a system that is not dominated by other disciplines (or theories), and on the other hand, to a system that is not dominated by educational theories from other countries. It emphasizes the subjectivity, national character, and Chinese characteristics of the educational discipline itself.
- In the process of developing the educational discipline in China, there have been varying degrees of insufficient “autonomy”—namely, insufficient subjectivity, national character, and Chinese identity. To some extent, there has been a problem of identity confusion or an identity crisis.
- An “independent” educational disciplinary system is not a “closed,” “exclusive,” or “narrow” educational disciplinary system.

## **The Sinicization of Educational Theory ≠ Original Chinese Educational Theory**
The concepts of “the Sinicization of educational theory” and “the localization of educational theory” carry, to varying degrees, certain so-called “colonial” implications and even retain traces of “Western-centrism,” because their premise remains external—more precisely, Western—educational theories that originated in foreign contexts. Their underlying logic is still to select, transform, and apply external educational theories in combination with local educational realities. During this process, external educational theories may generate new conflicts, raise new questions, and reconstruct new theories through their interaction with local educational practices. However, overall, this model is unlikely to produce “local theories” that truly embody Chinese originality (Qu et al., 2006).
