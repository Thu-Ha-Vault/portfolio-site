## Complex Cognitive Process - Learning Strategies and Self-regulation *
### Learning Strategies
- **Core Definition**: Learning strategies are complex schemes about learning processes that learners purposefully and consciously devise to improve learning effectiveness and efficiency
**Four Core Characteristics of Learning Strategies**
- **1. Proactivity**: Conscious psychological processes of learners, requiring analysis of task characteristics to formulate plans, especially for new tasks.
- **2. Effectiveness**: Aimed at improving learning efficiency, strategies can achieve better learning outcomes compared to primitive, time-consuming methods
- **3. Process-oriented**: Strategies concern learning processes, specifying "what to do", "when to do", and "how to do" in learning steps.
- **4. Procedural**: Strategies consist of rules and skills; similar learning tasks typically have fundamentally similar planning patterns.
### Classification Framework of Learning
- **Core Idea:** According to the classification by Mckeachie et al., learning strategies can be systematically divided into three major categories, which play different roles in learning activities and form a hierarchical organic whole.
- **1. Cognitive Strategies:** Methods and techniques directly used for information processing, serving as learning "tools".
	- Rehearsal Strategies: 
		- Definition: Reproducing material in working memory to maintain attention
		- Function: Maintaining information and preparing for deeper processing
		- Methods: Unintentional memorization (e.g., repeating numbers), intentional memorization (e.g., underlining, copying, repeated reading)
	- Elaboration Strategies:
		- Definition: Establishing connections between new and existing knowledge for deep processing
		- Function: Promoting understanding and helping transfer information to long-term memory
		- Methods: Using background knowledge, mnemonics, note-taking, generative questioning, analogy or summarization
	- Organization Strategies: Strategies that integrate learning materials and construct knowledge structures to facilitate understanding and memory.
		- Core Function: Reducing memory load and improving information retrieval efficiency by discovering relationships among information
		- Common Methods:
			1. Chunking: Combining scattered information into meaningful units
			2. Outlining: Organizing hierarchical structure and logical relationships of material
			3. Selecting Key Points: Identifying and marking core information
			4. Concept Maps: Graphically presenting concepts and their interrelationships.
- **2. Metacognitive Strategies:** Strategies for controlling and regulating information processing, serving as learning "commanders".
	- Definition of Metacognition: Proposed by Flavell, referring to "cognition about cognition", which is the knowledge about one's own cognitive processes and the ability to regulate these processes.
	- Core Metaphor: The "third eye" of thinking, constantly examining one's own learning activities
	- Dual Structure of Metacognition
		1. Metacognitive Knowledge: Stored in long-term memory, answering "what to know". Include knowledge about person, task, and strategies
		2. Metacognitive Control/Regulation: Mainly operates in working memory, answering "when/how to do". Responsible for management and control of cognitive behaviors.
			- Core Process of Metacognitive Control: 
				- Core Idea: Metacognitive control is a dynamic cyclical process that ensures learning activities proceed effectively toward goal
				- ![Ảnh chụp Màn hình 2026-09-14 lúc 22.55.05](/vault-assets/anh-chup-man-hinh-2026-09-14-luc-22-55-05-80069c1f.png)
	- Detailed Metacognitive Strategies: 
		- Planing Strategies:
			- Function of Planing Strategies: According to cognitive goals, systematically planning activities, predicting results, and selecting strategies before action to lay the foundation for successful learning tasks
				- **Core Analogy:** Like a football coach formulating tactics before a game, successful students set a "strategy" for each task.
			- Application Example of Planning Strategies:
				- **Task Analysis:** Predicting the time needed to complete assignments.
				- **Goal Setting:** Clarifying the level to be achieved in this learning session. 
				- **Strategy Selection:** Deciding between independent study or group learning.
				- **Resource Preparation:** Collecting and organizing relevant information before writing.
				- **Rehearsal Anticipation:** Systematically reviewing before exams and anticipating possible question types.
			- 
		- Monitoring Strategies:
			- **Function of Monitoring Strategies:** Real-time assessment and feedback of cognitive activity results and deficiencies during the learning process, like an **"alarm system"**.
			- **Core Function:** Making learners alert to problems in attention and comprehension for timely correction.
			- **Comprehension Monitoring**
				* Continuously asking oneself "Do I understand this content?" while reading.
				* Actively changing reading methods when aware of not understanding.
			- **Attention Focus**
				* Noticing when attention is dispersed.
				* Actively bringing it back to current learning tasks.
			- **Progress Monitoring**
				* Real-time monitoring of answering speed during exams.
				* Reasonable allocation of remaining time.
		* Regulation Strategies: 
			- **Function of Regulation Strategies:** Based on monitoring feedback, taking specific actions to correct learning behaviors and remedy comprehension deficiencies, which is key to ensuring ultimate achievement of learning goals.
			- **Core Characteristic:** Transformation from "problem identification" to "problem solving".
			- **Application Example of Regulation Strategies:**
				* **Background Case:** Student learning Sino-Japanese War history
				* **Planning:** Setting goals, using rehearsal and key points for learning
				* 🔍 **Monitoring:** Aware of difficulties when comparing wars and treaties
				* 🛠️ **Regulation:** Changed strategy, switched to simulating short-answer questions and writing answers to overcome difficulties
			- *Key Relationship: Regulation strategies are closely connected with monitoring strategies. Without effective monitoring, regulation has no basis.*
- **3. Resource Management Strategies:** Strategies that assist learners in managing environment and resources, serving as learning "logistics support".
	- **Definition:** Strategies that assist students in managing available environmental and internal resources to support and optimize the learning process.
	- *Core Position: Providing optimal "logistical support" for cognitive and metacognitive activities*
	- **Four Categories of Resource Management Strategies**
		- **1. Time Management Strategies:** Effectively planning and utilizing learning time. 
			- **Core Principle:** *Treat time as a precious resource for planning.*
			- **Specific Methods:**
				- Establish schedules
				- Set goals
				- Prioritize tasks
		- **2. Learning Environment Management Strategies:** Selecting and creating spaces with minimal distractions.
			- **Core Principle:** *Minimize distractions, maximize focus.*
			- **Specific Methods:**
				- Find a fixed and quiet location
				- Keep learning space organized
				- Eliminate potential distractions like phones
		- **3. Effort Management Strategies:** Stimulating and maintaining motivation, staying focused.
			* **Core Principle:** Regulating internal motivation and emotional states to cope with learning challenges.
			* **Specific Methods:** Positive attribution, enhancing confidence through self-talk, maintaining persistence.
		- **4. Academic Help-Seeking Strategies:** Effectively obtaining help from others when needed.
			- **Core Principle:** Viewing help-seeking as a positive learning strategy, not a sign of incompetence.
			* **Specific Methods:** Seeking teacher help, discussing with peers, utilizing teaching assistants or tutoring resources.

### Integration: What is Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)? 
- **Definition of Self-Regulated Learning:** A dynamic learning process in which learners actively motivate themselves and actively, systematically use learning strategies (including cognitive, metacognitive, and resource management strategies) to achieve personal goals. (→ [AI in Education](/blog/artificial-intelligence-in-education/))
- **Core Distinction:** Not only possessing strategy knowledge, but also having the willingness and ability to actively apply strategies in appropriate contexts.
* **Proactivity:** Masters of learning, not passive recipients.
* **Goal-oriented:** Setting clear, specific goals for themselves.
* **Strategic:** Rich strategy repertoire and flexible selection and adjustment.
* **High Self-efficacy:** Believing that effort leads to success.
### Three-Phase Cyclical Model of Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman)
*Core Idea: Self-regulated learning is a cyclical process that includes planning, execution, and reflection.*
* **1. Forethought/Planning Phase:** Preparation before learning. Includes task analysis, goal setting, strategy planning, and motivational beliefs.
	-  **Phase Core:** Thorough psychological and strategic preparation before learning begins.
	- **Task Analysis**
		- **Goal Setting:** Breaking down grand tasks into specific sub-goals.
		- **Strategic Planning:** Selecting strategy combinations based on goals and difficulty.
	- **Self-Motivational Beliefs**
		- **Self-Efficacy:** Confidence in one's ability to complete tasks.
		- **Outcome Expectations:** Anticipation of possible results after success.
		- **Intrinsic Interest/Value:** Interest in and recognition of content itself.
		- **Learning Goal Orientation:** Tendency to master knowledge and enhance abilities.
* **2. Performance/Volitional Phase:** Execution and monitoring during learning. Core focus is on self-control and real-time self-observation. **Putting Plans into Practice**
	* **Self-Control:** Using techniques to maintain progress, such as imagery or self-instruction.
	* **Self-Observation:** Observing learning behaviors like a researcher and recording strategy effectiveness.
* **3. Self-Reflection Phase:** Evaluation and adaptation after learning. Includes self-judgment of results and causal attribution. **Evaluation and Cyclical Adjustment**
	* **Self-Judgment:** Evaluating against goals and analyzing causal attributions for success or failure.
	* **Self-Reaction:** Generating emotional feedback based on judgments and deciding subsequent adaptive actions.

# Classical Developmental Theories II
## Constructivist Learning Theory - Classical Theory 
### **The "Inertness" of Learning and the Emergence of Constructivism**
- *Core Reflection* : Why is it difficult for school knowledge to be flexibly applied in reality? Knowledge from traditional teaching has problems such as "incompleteness", "inertness", and "inflexibility".
- **Constructivist Answer** : *Learning is not the passive reception of information, but an active process of meaning construction by learners.* (→ [Major Schools of Educational Theory](/blog/the-major-schools-of-educational-theory/))
- **Two Core Viewpoints:**
	* Learners can actively construct their own knowledge.
	* Social interaction is important in the knowledge construction process.
### Epistemological Foundations of Constructivism
- *Core Question*: *How is universal and necessary knowledge acquired?*
- *[Empiricism] Objectivism*  
	- Representative: Locke's "Tabula Rasa"  
	- Viewpoint: Knowledge comes from sensory experience of the external world, and knowledge pre-exists in the object.
- *[Rationalism/A Priori] Subjectivism*  
	- Representative: Leibniz's "Innate Ideas"  
	- Viewpoint: Knowledge pre-exists in the inner world, and the universality of knowledge is innate.
- **Kant's Reconciliation:** *Knowledge arises from the combination of empirical materials and "transcendental self" categories.*
- *Piaget's Transcendence (Core of Constructivism):* *Knowledge originates from the* **activity process** *that connects the interaction between subject and object.*
### Principle I: View of Knowledge - Knowledge as Relative and Dynamically Constructed Interpretation
- **【Constructivist View of Knowledge】** *Knowledge is not an accurate representation of reality, but an interpretation, a hypothesis. It continuously "innovates" with human progress and has tentative nature.*
- **Scientific Domain: Dynamic Evolution**: Human understanding of the smallest unit of matter: atom → nucleus → proton + neutron → quarks. Newton's laws were revised by relativity in macroscopic/high-speed scales.
- **Humanities Domain: Subjective Interpretation**
  Individual understanding depends on original experience background. As Lu Xun said: "In one 'Dream of the Red Chamber', scholars see 'I Ching', moralists see lewdness, talents see romance..."
- *Core Characteristics:* *Questioning the objectivity and certainty of knowledge, emphasizing the dynamic growth of knowledge.*
### Principle II: View of Learning - Active Construction, Social Interaction and Situatedness
- **Active Constructiveness**: Learning is not a simple transfer of knowledge from outside to inside, but an active process where learners transform their knowledge through interaction between new experiences and existing knowledge experiences.
- **Social Interactivity**: Learning is a process of internalizing knowledge and skills through participation in certain sociocultural activities, often requiring collaborative assistance from learning communities to complete.
- **Situatedness**: Knowledge exists in concrete and perceptible activities. Knowledge separated from activity contexts is difficult to truly understand and apply. Learning should be combined with situated social practices.
### How is Knowledge Constructed? - Three Perspectives Compared
- *Debates on the Sources of Knowledge Construction:* *Three Explanatory Frameworks Proposed by Moshman (1982)*

| Type | Assumptions about Learning and Knowledge | Representative Theory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **External Construction** | Knowledge is acquired by constructing accurate mental representations of the external world. Knowledge is a reflection of objective reality. | Information Processing Theory |
| **Internal Construction** | Knowledge is constructed through transforming, organizing and reorganizing prior knowledge. It is the result of cognitive activity rather than a mirror of the external world. | Piaget's Theory |
| **Internal-External Co-construction** | Knowledge is co-constructed based on social interaction and experience. It both reflects the external world and is influenced by internal factors. | Vygotsky's Theory |
### Two Major Waves of Constructivism: Individual and Social
- *Core Viewpoint* : Although both recognize learners' active construction of knowledge, they have different emphases.
- **First Wave: Individual Constructivism**
	* **Representative** : Piaget
	* **Focus** : Individual's "inside the mind"
	* **Core Mechanism** : Focus on how individuals use information and resources to construct and perfect mental models and strategies.
	- *Focus:* How individuals understand the external world based on their own knowledge, beliefs, and self-concepts.
	- **Piaget's Psychological Constructivist View**
		- **Sources of Knowledge:** *Universal knowledge structures originate from reflection and coordination of one's own cognition and thinking, rather than direct learning from the environment.*
		- **Construction Process:** *Bidirectional action of assimilation and accommodation. New and old experiences interact to form, enrich and adjust individual cognitive structures.*
		- **Role of Social Interaction:** *Social environment is an important factor for development, but not the "primary mechanism" for cognitive change.*
	- ***Extreme Form: Radical Constructivism***
		- **Representative** : Von Glasersfeld  
		- *This is a further development of Piaget's genetic epistemology, considered an extreme form of constructivism.*
			**1. Active Construction Principle**: *Knowledge is actively constructed by cognitive subjects through new and old experiences.*
			**2. Adaptability Principle**: *Cognition is to adapt to the experiential world to help "survival", not to discover truth.
		- *True learning occurs when the subject encounters* **"adaptive difficulties"**, *when learning motivation is maximally activated.*
		- *Theoretical Limitations* : Denying the role of social interaction, prone to solipsism and relativism, opposing unified knowledge standards.
	- ***Individual Constructivism: Generative Learning Theory***
		- **Generative Learning Theory (Wittrock)** : Learning is the process by which learners actively construct internal mental representations based on prior knowledge and experience.
		- **Generative Learning Process** : Learners interact with sensory information received from the environment through knowledge and strategies stored in long-term memory, actively attending to and selecting information, and generating meaning from information.
		- *Generative Learning Model Diagram*: ![Ảnh chụp Màn hình 2026-09-14 lúc 23.35.28](/vault-assets/anh-chup-man-hinh-2026-09-14-luc-23-35-28-dfb5c5da.png)
	- ***Individual Constructivism: Cognitive Flexibility Theory***
		- **Cognitive Flexibility Theory (Spiro)** : Explains how to promote flexible transfer and application of knowledge through deepening understanding, aiming to cultivate learners' adaptive response capabilities to deal with complex and irregular real situations.
		- **Well-Structured Domains & Introductory Acquisition**
			* **Domain Features** : Facts, concepts, rules with clear hierarchical structure.
			* **Learning Features** : Focused on memory and reproduction, attention to knowledge as is.
			* **Typical Tasks** : Recitation, fill-in-the-blank, completing structured exercises.
		- **Ill-Structured Domains & Advanced Acquisition**
			* **Domain Features** : Generated in specific contexts, concepts are complex and irregular.
			* **Learning Features** : Require application in multiple contexts, grasping interconnections.
			* **Typical Tasks** : Solving complex real problems, achieving cross-contextual transfer.
- **Second Wave: Social Constructivism**
	* **Representative** : Vygotsky
	* **Focus** : Interaction between individual and society
	* **Core Mechanism** : Views learning as a process of enhancing capabilities through cooperation with others and participation in culturally meaningful activities.
	* ***Social Constructivism Core Principles***
		* **Focus**: *Sociocultural mechanisms behind learning and knowledge construction*
		- **Vygotsky's Viewpoints**
			* **Core Mechanism** : The development of higher mental functions is the result of sociocultural internalization.
			* **Learning Process** : Learning is a cultural participation process where learners use cultural support (such as language and tools) to participate in learning community practices and internalize relevant knowledge.
			* **Role of Social Interaction** : Social interaction is the core of learning; higher mental functions (such as reasoning) develop in social interactions and are then internalized by individuals.
	- ***Key Mechanisms of Social Constructivism***
		- *Cultural Internalization*: The process by which individuals transform culture in society (such as language and conceptual systems) into part of their own internal psychological structure and use it to consciously guide their psychological activities.
			- *Internalization Path* : Development follows the pattern from social level to individual level.
		- *Activity Theory*: Activity is the bridge of the internalization process. A sensory practice process of interaction between subject and object.
			- *Role of Activity* : People form knowledge by reflecting the objective world through activity, and also test and develop knowledge by acting on the objective world through activity.
*Note: Both recognize learners' agency, with main differences in the sources of knowledge and the role of social interaction.*
### Situated Cognition and Situated Learning Theory
- **Situated Cognition**: Believes that human cognition is inseparable from its physical, social and cultural context, and "knowing" and "acting" are unified.
- **Situated Learning**: Knowledge emerges from activities in specific cultural contexts, and its meaning depends on the activities, contexts and cultures in which it is applied.
- **Examples: Meaning Influenced by Context**
	- *Activity Influence:*
		* "This river is hard to cross" (difficulty crossing)
		* "Failing the exam is so sad" (emotional sadness)
	- *Cultural Influence:*
		* Number "8": China (lucky) vs West (ordinary)
		* Number "13": Taboo in Western culture
### Distributed Cognition
- *Core Concept:* Cognition is distributed among individuals, between individuals, media, culture, society and time.
* **Mental vs. Written Calculation:** Written calculation is distributed between mind and paper, reducing the burden of working memory.
* **Partner Memory Distribution:** Wives share the content that husbands need to remember (such as daily chores).
* **Restaurant Dining Context:** Cognition is distributed among menus, rules, and staff division of labor.
- **Distributed Learning and Culture**: Teaching occurs independently of time and space, which is of great significance for collaborative learning and distance education.
- **Technology and Cognitive Tools**: Focus shifts from "how to draw" to "how to use charts to express data meaning".
### Distinction: Social Constructivism vs. Social Constructionism
- **Core Clarification:** *Both focus on social factors, but analyze at different levels.*
- **[Social Constructivism]**
	* ● Theoretical Foundation: Vygotsky's theory
	* ● Focus: *Individual learning*
	* ● Core Logic: Exploring how social interactions and cultural tools shape "individual" development and learning.
- **[Social Constructionism]**
	* ● Theoretical Foundation: Philosophical thought (Derrida, etc.)
	* ● Focus: *Construction of public knowledge*
	* ● Core Logic: Studying how disciplinary knowledge is socially constructed and how power relations influence legitimacy.
### Summary: Learner-Centered Psychological Principles (APA, 1997)
- *Comprehensive Embodiment* : These principles outline a learner image of active inquiry and social construction of meaning.
- **Core Principles Extract (Cognitive and Metacognitive Factors)**
	* **Nature of Learning Process** : Process of actively constructing meaning
	* **Knowledge Construction** : Meaningful connections between new information and existing knowledge
	* **Learning Context** : Influenced by cultural, technological and environmental factors
	* **Developmental Influence** : Should comprehensively consider physical and mental developmental characteristics
	* **Social Influence** : Social interaction and interpersonal communication both affect learning
	* **Individual Differences** : Genetics and experience lead to different learning strategies
