## Cell Commitment
- **Differentiation**: the generation of specialized cell types – only the last, overt stage of a series of events that commit a blastomere to a particular fate.
- Commitment has two stages:
	- **Specification** – labile: the cell differentiates autonomously when placed in a **neutral environment** (petri dish); still **reversible**.
	- **Determination** – the cell differentiates autonomously even when placed in **another region of the embryo**; commitment is assumed **irreversible**.

### Three modes of specification
- **Autonomous (mosaic) specification**
	- The blastomere inherits **morphogenetic determinants** (transcription factors or their mRNAs) from the non-homogeneous egg cytoplasm.
	- The cell "knows" its fate early, without interacting with other cells.
	- Example: **trochoblast** (ciliated) cells of the mollusk *Patella* – isolated at the 16-cell stage, they still divide and become ciliated on schedule in culture.
- **Conditional specification**
	- Fate depends on **position** in the embryo and on interactions with neighbors (**paracrine factors**).
	- If cells are removed, the remaining cells can **regulate** and compensate for the missing part. (→ [Phát triển phôi sớm ở chim](/blog/sinh-hoc/biology-project-and-lab-report/developmental-biology/01-su-phat-trien-phoi-som-o-chim/))
- **Syncytial specification**
	- Uses elements of both. In early insect embryos nuclei divide without cell division → a **syncytium** (many nuclei in one cytoplasm).
	- Anterior and posterior nuclei are exposed to different cytoplasmic transcription factors.

## Developmental Genetics
- **Genomic equivalence**: every somatic nucleus descends mitotically from the zygote → same chromosomes, same genes.
- **Differential gene expression**: unused genes are neither destroyed nor mutated; only a small fraction of the genome is expressed in each cell type. (→ [Điều hoà hoạt động gen](/blog/sinh-hoc/kien-thuc-chung/b-co-che-di-truyen-va-bien-di/05-dieu-hoa-hoat-dong-gen/))
- Levels of regulation:
	1. **Differential gene transcription** – which genes are transcribed into nuclear RNA.
	2. **Selective nuclear RNA processing** – which transcripts (or parts) reach the cytoplasm as mRNA.
	3. **Selective mRNA translation** – which mRNAs are translated.
	4. **Differential protein modification** – which proteins remain or function.

## Differential Gene Transcription
### Nucleosome and histone regulation
- **Chromatin** = DNA + protein (about half the weight, mostly **histones**).
- **Nucleosome**: histone octamer (2 × H2A, H2B, H3, H4) wrapped by ~140 bp of DNA; **histone H1** binds ~60 bp of linker DNA.
- Histone tails of H3 and H4 are modified to repress or activate transcription:
	- **Histone acetylation** (histone acetyltransferases) neutralizes the positive lysine charge → loosens nucleosomes → **activates** transcription. **Histone deacetylases** reverse this → repression.
	- **Histone methylation** (histone methyltransferases) can activate or repress depending on the residue and neighboring marks.
- **Epigenetic memory**:
	- **Polycomb** proteins keep genes **off**: one set methylates **H3K27** and **H3K9**; a second set binds the methylated tails and spreads methylation → tightly packed repressive chromatin.
	- **Trithorax** proteins keep genes **on**, countering Polycomb.

### Anatomy of the gene
- **Promoter**: binding site of RNA polymerase, immediately upstream of the start site; often contains the **TATA box** ~30 bp upstream.
- **Enhancer**: controls efficiency and rate of transcription from a promoter; binds transcription factors that recruit histone-modifying enzymes or stabilize the initiation complex. Acts only on **cis-linked** promoters (cis-regulatory element).
- **Transcription factors** have three domains: **DNA-binding**, **trans-activating**, and **protein–protein interaction**.
- **Pioneer transcription factors** penetrate repressed chromatin: **FoxA** (liver), **Pax7** (muscle stem cells), **Pbx** (helps **MyoD**).
- **Silencer** ("negative enhancer"): e.g. **NRSE** binds **NRSF**, a zinc-finger factor expressed in every cell except mature neurons → neural genes are repressed outside the nervous system.

### DNA methylation
- Promoters of inactive genes are methylated at **CpG** cytosines → **5-methylcytosine** (~5% of cytosines in mammals).
	- Example: the β-globin gene is unmethylated (active) in red blood cell precursors but methylated in fibroblasts.
- Mechanisms of repression: blocks transcription factor binding; recruits proteins that deacetylate or methylate histones.
- Inheritance: **MeCP2** binds methylated C and recruits **Dnmt3** (de novo methylation); **Dnmt1** copies the pattern onto the new strand after replication (needs the C to be next to a G). (→ [Nhân đôi DNA](/blog/sinh-hoc/kien-thuc-chung/b-co-che-di-truyen-va-bien-di/04-nhan-doi-dna-phien-ma-va-dich-ma/))

## Differential RNA Processing
- **Nuclear RNA selection**: the same pool of nuclear transcripts gives different cytoplasmic mRNA populations in different cell types.
- **Differential (alternative) RNA splicing**: different combinations of exons from one pre-mRNA → a family of proteins from a single gene (e.g. exons 1-2-4-5 in one cell type, 1-2-3 in another).
