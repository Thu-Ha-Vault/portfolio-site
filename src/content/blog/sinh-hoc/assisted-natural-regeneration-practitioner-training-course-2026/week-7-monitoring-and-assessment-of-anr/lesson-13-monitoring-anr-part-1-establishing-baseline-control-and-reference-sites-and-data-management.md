## Establishing Baseline and Control Sites, Data Management

## Learning Objectives
After this lesson, learners should be able to:
- Understand baseline, control site, reference site, and sampling design concepts.
- Select appropriate baseline, control, and reference conditions.
- Apply good practices for sampling and data quality assurance.

---

# 1. Key Concepts: Baseline, Control Areas, Reference Sites and Sampling Design

## 1.1 Site Selection and Baseline Inventory Process

ANR monitoring begins long before restoration activities.

The process follows four stages:

### Step 1. Initial Site Assessment
Purpose:
- Understand degradation level
- Identify degradation drivers
- Evaluate ecological and socio-economic conditions
- Decide whether ANR is suitable

### Step 2. Site Selection

Choose:
- ANR treatment sites
- Control sites
- Reference sites

Site selection may be delayed if natural regeneration must first be observed.

### Step 3. Baseline Inventory

Collect detailed ecological and social information before any intervention. (→ [L5 Biophysical assessments](/blog/sinh-hoc/assisted-natural-regeneration-practitioner-training-course-2026/week-3-conducting-baseline-site-and-landscape-assessments/lesson-5-conducting-biophysical-assessments-at-site-and-landscape-scale/))

This dataset becomes the benchmark for future comparison.

### Step 4. Adaptive Refinement

Site selection may be modified after:
- Baseline inventory
- Additional regeneration observations

---

# 2. Baseline

## Definition

Baseline = condition of the restoration site immediately before ANR begins.

Includes:

### Ecological attributes
- Vegetation
- Soil
- Species composition
- Ecosystem function

### Social attributes
- Livelihood
- Community use
- Cultural values

---

## Baseline Inventory

Documents:

- Species composition
- Vegetation structure
- Ecosystem functions
- Abiotic conditions

Purpose:
- Develop restoration goals
- Build reference model
- Design treatment strategy

---

## Why Collect Baseline Data?

Without baseline:

- No "before" condition
- Impossible to measure restoration success
- Cannot attribute improvements to ANR

Baseline acts as the project's benchmark. (→ [TLTK Thực tập thiên nhiên](/blog/sinh-hoc/biology-project-and-lab-report/biological-field-study/tai-lieu-tham-khao-thuc-tap-thien-nhien/))

---

## Baseline Monitoring Best Practices

Measure indicators of:

### Ecological
- Abiotic conditions
- Species composition
- Vegetation structure
- Ecosystem function
- Connectivity
- Threats

### Social
- Cultural values
- Livelihood
- Socio-economic indicators

Important principles:

- Match indicators with project objectives.
- Use same indicators throughout monitoring.
- Sample both treatment and future control plots.
- Follow standardized protocols.
- Complete before any restoration activity.

---

# 3. Control Areas

## Definition

Control areas are untreated sites located near ANR sites. (→ [Causal Inference](/blog/sinh-hoc/bioinformatics/a-statistics-and-biostatistics/iii-statistical-models-and-method/04-causal-inference/))

Purpose:
Provide comparison to distinguish:

ANR effects

vs.

Natural environmental changes.

---

## Why Are Control Areas Important?

They help:

- Measure ANR effectiveness
- Compare ANR vs natural regeneration
- Assess cost-effectiveness
- Avoid overestimating project success
- Support evidence-based management

---

## Good Control Site Characteristics

Control sites should:

- Have similar soil
- Similar topography
- Similar disturbance history
- Similar socio-economic context
- Experience similar climate

But be sufficiently distant to avoid ANR influence.

Typical distance:

Small homogeneous landscapes:
50–100 m

Large heterogeneous landscapes:
100–500 m

---

## Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Different soil types
- Different land-use history
- Controls too close to ANR
- Losing control sites because of later land-use changes

---

# 4. Reference Sites

## Definition

Reference sites are relatively intact ecosystems representing the desired future condition.

They are used to build a reference model.

---

## Roles

Reference sites help:

- Define restoration goals
- Evaluate recovery progress
- Guide management
- Support adaptive management

---

## Selection Criteria

Reference sites should have:

- Similar vegetation
- Similar soils
- Similar hydrology
- Healthy ecosystem processes
- Minimal disturbance
- Same landscape context

---

# 5. Sampling Design

Sampling design determines:

- Where to sample
- How many samples
- When to sample

Goal:
Reliable and statistically representative monitoring. (→ [Statistic and Regression](/blog/ai-va-dev/khoa-hoc-ai-cua-tsing-hua-university/learning-module/05-statistic-and-regression/))

---

## Key Principles

Use:

- Multiple plots
- Standardized methods
- Random or systematic placement
- Equal treatment and control sampling

---

## Common Sampling Designs

### Random Sampling

Random plot placement.

Advantages:
- Reduces bias.

---

### Systematic Sampling

Plots placed at regular intervals.

Advantages:
- Easy implementation.

---

### Stratified Sampling

Separate landscape into homogeneous units.

Best for:
- Variable landscapes

---

### Nested Sampling

Large plots contain smaller subplots. (→ [Rừng ngập mặn Phù Long](/blog/sinh-hoc/biology-project-and-lab-report/biological-field-study/06-co-so-sinh-thai-hoc-rung-ngap-man-phu-long/))

Best for forests.

Example:

Trees:
20 × 20 m

Seedlings:
5 × 5 m

---

## Plot Types

Common plot designs include:

- Square quadrats
- Circular plots
- Transects (→ [Giáp xác](/blog/sinh-hoc/biology-project-and-lab-report/biological-field-study/05-dvkxs-duoi-nuoc-giap-xac/))
- Stratified plots

---

# 6. Multiple Baseline Approaches

## Direct Baseline

Collected before restoration.

Advantages:

- Most accurate
- Strong before-after comparison

Preferred for all ANR projects.

---

## Retrospective Baseline

Estimated after restoration begins.

Sources:

- Satellite imagery
- Historical photos
- Reports
- Local knowledge

Less accurate.

---

## Proxy Baseline

Uses nearby similar sites when original baseline is unavailable.

Must have similar:

- Soil
- Climate
- Land-use history
- Degradation level

---

# 7. Sampling for Site Variability

One baseline survey may include many sampling units. (→ [Cá, lưỡng cư, bò sát](/blog/sinh-hoc/biology-project-and-lab-report/biological-field-study/03-dvcxs-duoi-nuoc-ca-luong-cu-bo-sat/))

Sampling should represent:

- Soil variation
- Topography
- Microclimate
- Disturbance history
- ANR methods

Highly variable sites should be divided into smaller monitoring units.

---

# 8. Multiple Control Designs

## Paired Control Design

Each ANR plot paired with one nearby control.

Simple and effective.

---

## BACI Design

Before–After–Control–Impact

Compares:

Before vs After

AND

Treatment vs Control

Most robust design for evaluating ANR impact.

---

# 9. Reference Models

Reference models describe the desired ecosystem.

Usually developed using:

- Multiple reference sites
- Historical information
- Ecological knowledge
- Remote sensing

---

## Six Ecosystem Attributes

A good reference model includes:

1. Absence of threats
2. Physical conditions
3. Species composition
4. Structural diversity
5. Ecosystem function
6. External exchanges

---

## Multiple Reference Models

Needed when:

- Large landscapes
- Multiple ecosystem types
- Climate change affects future conditions

---

## Rehabilitation Caveat

For rehabilitation projects (e.g. Farmer Managed Natural Regeneration):

Reference ecosystems are NOT project goals.

Instead:

They represent biodiversity ideals.

Monitoring can rely mainly on paired controls or BACI.

---

# 10. Why, When and How to Measure

## Why?

Monitoring evaluates:

- Effectiveness
- Ecological impacts
- Socio-economic impacts
- Implementation quality
- Adaptive management
- Reporting

---

## When?

Recommended schedule:

Baseline:
Before intervention

Early:
6–12 months

Intermediate:
1–3 years

Long-term:
5+ years

Always monitor during the same season.

---

## How?

Best practice:

- Clear objectives
- Paired controls
- Replicated plots
- Permanent plots
- GPS coordinates
- Consistent plot sizes
- Standard methods
- Metadata recording
- Statistical analysis

Comparisons:

- Before vs After
- ANR vs Control
- ANR vs Reference

---

# 11. Reference Model Options

Highest integrity ecosystems are preferred:

Examples:

- Old-growth forest
- Mature secondary forest

If unavailable:

Supplement with:

- Historical records
- Climate models
- Remote sensing
- Local knowledge

---

# 12. Control Site Options

Preferred:

Unassisted natural regeneration.

Alternative options:

- Chronosequences
- Recovery gradients
- Different intervention intensities

---

# 13. Ensuring Data Quality

Monitoring consists of six stages:

1. Planning
2. Preparation
3. Data collection
4. Data management (→ [Prepare Data for Exploration](/blog/sinh-hoc/bioinformatics/b-data-science-and-bioinformatics/i-data-science/03-prepare-data-for-exploration/))
5. Analysis and reporting
6. Adaptive monitoring

---

## Quality Assurance Actions

Planning
- Define objectives
- Select indicators
- Standardize protocols

Preparation
- Train field teams
- Establish permanent plots

Collection
- Standardized measurements
- Metadata recording
- Field verification

Data Management
- Database entry
- Validation
- Cleaning

Analysis
- Compare baseline, control and reference
- Appropriate statistics

Adaptive Monitoring
- Improve methods
- Retrain teams
- Continue long-term monitoring (→ [L14 Monitoring part 2](/blog/sinh-hoc/assisted-natural-regeneration-practitioner-training-course-2026/week-7-monitoring-and-assessment-of-anr/lesson-14-monitoring-anr-part-2-assessing-outcomes-and-adaptive-management/))

# 14. Case Studies

## Burkina Faso

Paired control design:

- 27 grazing exclusion sites
- Circular plots
- Controls located 100 m away

Measured:

- Tree diversity
- Stem density
- DBH

Result:

ANR sites had higher diversity and density.

---

## Australia

Three site types:

- Grazed control
- Ungrazed
- Ungrazed + invasive species control

Measured:

- Canopy cover
- Species richness
- Stem density
- Recruitment

Result:

Ungrazed treatments outperformed grazed controls.

---

# Key Takeaways

Successful ANR monitoring depends on:

- Strong baseline data
- Appropriate control sites
- Reliable reference models
- Representative sampling
- High-quality data
- Long-term monitoring
- Adaptive management
