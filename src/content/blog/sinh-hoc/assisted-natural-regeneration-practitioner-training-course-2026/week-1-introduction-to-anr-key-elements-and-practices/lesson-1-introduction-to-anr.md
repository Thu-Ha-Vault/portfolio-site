## Overview
Assisted Natural Regeneration (ANR) is a forest restoration approach that accelerates the recovery of degraded forests by supporting natural ecological processes rather than relying primarily on tree planting. ANR focuses on protecting and nurturing existing seedlings, sprouts, and seed sources while removing barriers that prevent forest recovery.

## Key Concepts

### What is ANR?
- A restoration method that works with nature instead of replacing it.
- Relies on naturally occurring regeneration from seeds, rootstocks, and surviving vegetation.
- Uses targeted interventions to help forests recover more quickly.

### Main Objectives
- Restore degraded forest landscapes.
- Improve biodiversity and ecosystem functions.
- Increase carbon sequestration and climate resilience.
- Support sustainable livelihoods for local communities.

### Core Principles
1. Protect existing natural regeneration.
2. Remove barriers to forest recovery.
3. Encourage native species growth.
4. Use enrichment planting only when necessary.

## Benefits of ANR

### Environmental Benefits
- Restores native biodiversity.
- Improves soil fertility and structure.
- Enhances watershed protection.
- Increases carbon storage. (→ [Hệ sinh thái và sinh quyển](/blog/sinh-hoc/kien-thuc-chung/f-sinh-thai-hoc/21-he-sinh-thai-va-sinh-quyen/))

### Economic Benefits
- Lower cost than conventional tree planting.
- Reduced need for nurseries and planting operations.
- Scalable across large restoration areas.

### Social Benefits
- Encourages community participation.
- Builds local stewardship of natural resources.
- Supports long-term sustainable land management.

## Key Takeaways
- ANR accelerates natural forest recovery through targeted management.
- It is often more cost-effective than large-scale tree planting.
- Success depends on protecting existing vegetation and reducing disturbances.
- ANR combines ecological effectiveness with practical implementation.
