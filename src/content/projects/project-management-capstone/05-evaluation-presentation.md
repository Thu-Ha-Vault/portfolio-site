> Slide-by-slide outline. The course states that **36%** of guest respondents reported a negative dining experience with the tablets; the other figures are illustrative, marked *(illustrative)*, because the course dataset is not in the vault.

**Slide 1: Purpose.** The pilot tests whether tabletop tablets help two Sauce & Spoon locations serve more guests in less time without hurting the guest experience. Today: results of the launch-testing milestone (tablets installed, integrated with the POS system, staff trained) and a decision we need.

**Slide 2: Headline.** Staff work faster with the tablets, but **36% of guests said the tablets made their visit worse**. Speed goals are on track; guest experience is not.

**Slide 3: What went well** *(illustrative)*
- Average checkout time: **−12%** (goal −10%)
- Order accuracy: **97.6%** of orders without correction (goal 98%)
- Staff compliance with the tablet process: **93%** (goal > 90%)

**Slide 4: The problem.** 36% of guest respondents reported a negative experience. Open-text answers point to three causes *(illustrative shares of the negative comments)*:
- Tablet froze or lost the order: 41%
- Not sure how to order or call a server: 34%
- Prefer ordering with a person: 25%

**Slide 5: What it means.** Two of the three causes are fixable by the project: a software issue (freezes) and onboarding (guests not shown how to use the tablet). The third is a preference that the pilot should respect, not override.

**Slide 6: Recommendations**
1. Vendor patch for the freeze issue, tested before the next weekend service (owner: tablet vendor; due: 1 week).
2. Servers greet every new table with a 15-second tablet demo and offer to take the order (owner: general managers; due: immediately).
3. Add a "call server" button to the home screen (owner: vendor; due: 2 weeks).
4. Re-survey for two weeks; continue the pilot if negative experiences fall below 15%.

**Slide 7: Decision needed.** Approve the vendor patch within the existing contingency budget and the two-week extension of the guest survey.

**Speaker notes.** Lead with the goal, show only the data that changes a decision, and offer interpretations of the 36% rather than a single cause.
