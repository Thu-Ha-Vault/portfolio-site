> Results after the six-week pilot. Figures marked *(illustrative)* show the structure of the report; the course provides its own data.

## Executive summary
**Project vision.** Sauce & Spoon wants to serve working families fresh food quickly. The pilot tested whether tabletop tablets at two of five locations make service faster and more accurate without hurting the guest experience, as evidence for a chain-wide decision.

**Key accomplishments**
- Delivered all six charter deliverables in **17 weeks** and within budget (**$43,900 of $44,500**, including the $1,800 software patch) *(illustrative)*.
- Checkout time **−12%** (goal −10%); peak-hour table turnover **+9%** (goal +8%) *(illustrative)*.
- Order accuracy **98.3%** after the patch (goal 98%); staff compliance **94%** *(illustrative)*.
- Negative guest experience fell from **36%** at launch testing to **11%** after the patch and the table-side demo *(illustrative after the first figure)*.

**Lessons learned**
- Turn vague goals into checkable standards early ("easy to use" → order in ≤ 20 seconds).
- Include the people closest to the work (kitchen managers) in regular check-ins from the start.
- Train staff on how to introduce a new tool to guests, not only how to use it.
- One owner per vendor process and a shared issue log prevent small problems from becoming escalations.

## Recommendation
Roll out tablets to the remaining three locations in two waves, using the patched software, the table-side demo and the kitchen-manager check-in as standard. Keep waitstaff at every table; revisit online ordering as a separate project using the pilot's ordering data.
