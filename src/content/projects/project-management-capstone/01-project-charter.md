## Project summary
Sauce & Spoon, a five-location restaurant chain serving fresh, quick food to working families, will pilot **tabletop menu tablets** at **two locations**. Guests will browse the menu, order and pay from the table. The pilot tests whether tablets let the restaurants serve more guests in less time and collect useful data, before a decision on rolling them out to all five locations.

## Project goals (SMART)
| # | Goal | Measure |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Decrease average customer **checkout time by 10%** in the first **six weeks** after launch at both pilot locations | POS timestamps: bill requested → payment complete, compared with the six weeks before launch |
| 2 | Increase **order accuracy** to **98%** of orders without a correction within six weeks of launch | kitchen remake log / total orders |
| 3 | Increase **table turnover** at peak hours (11:30–13:30, 18:00–20:00) by **8%** within six weeks of launch | covers per table per peak period |
| 4 | Reach **90% staff compliance** with the tablet ordering process by week 4 after launch | manager spot checks, twice weekly |
| 5 | Keep guest satisfaction at or above the pre-launch level (**≥ 4.2 / 5**) during the pilot | in-restaurant guest survey |

Goals 1–3 serve the company objective "run an efficient, profitable business model"; goals 4–5 protect "prioritize our customer needs".

## Deliverables
1. Tablet vendor selected and contract signed
2. 60 tablets installed at the two pilot locations *(assumption: about 30 tables each)* and integrated with the POS system
3. Digital menu built and tested (ordering, modifiers, allergens, payment)
4. Training delivered to management, front-of-house and back-of-house staff, with role-specific materials
5. Launch promotion: table signs and an email blast to loyalty members
6. Evaluation: guest and staff surveys, data report and recommendation on a chain-wide rollout

## Scope
| In scope | Out of scope |
| --- | --- |
| Tablets at 2 of 5 locations | The other 3 locations (decided after the pilot) |
| Ordering, re-ordering drinks, paying at the table | **Fully automating** service or reducing waitstaff |
| POS integration and digital menu | Online / delivery ordering and a mobile app |
| Staff training and launch promotion | Kitchen display system replacement |
| Six-week post-launch evaluation | Loyalty program redesign |

Stakeholders disagreed early: one wanted the tablets to **fully automate ordering over time**, another wanted them to **improve order accuracy**. Agreement reached: the pilot measures speed and accuracy with waitstaff still at every table; automation is out of scope and can be proposed after the evaluation.

## Benefits and costs
| Benefits | Costs *(assumption)* |
| --- | --- |
| Faster checkout and higher table turnover at peak | Tablets and charging cases: about $24,000 |
| Fewer wrong orders and remakes (less food waste) | POS integration and menu build: about $9,000 |
| Data on ordering patterns, best-sellers and peak times | Training (vendor + staff hours): about $6,000 |
| Servers spend more time on hospitality, less on order taking | Signage and email promotion: about $1,500 |
| Evidence for a chain-wide decision with low risk (2 sites) | Contingency 10%: about $4,000 · **Total about $44,500** |

## Stakeholders
| Role | Interest in the project |
| --- | --- |

| Finance lead | budget, cost per tablet, payback period |
| Director of operations | service speed, staffing, consistency across locations |
| General managers, 2 pilot locations | daily operations, guest experience, staff buy-in |
| Kitchen managers, 2 pilot locations | order accuracy, ticket flow into the kitchen |
| Front- and back-of-house staff | ease of use, tips, workload |
| Marketing lead | launch promotion, guest communication |
| Tablet vendor, POS provider | delivery, integration, support |
| Project manager (Peta) | scope, schedule, budget, quality |
